CONTEXT: A maker inspects their own rendered image dozens of times across many
rounds. Measured in-house: the zones that were looked at MOST are the ones where
first-order defects survived longest; a break of about an hour does not restore the
eye; and being told what an object is ("this is a pillow") appears to suppress
detection of its form errors. We already run fresh-eyes rotation, blind ranking and
forced-choice instruments. What we lack is the literature's mechanism names, effect
SIZES, and parameters for the countermeasures. Effect sizes must carry a citation
with DOI or a direct paper URL; an uncited number is useless to us.

QUESTIONS:

1. What does the visual-perception literature name and quantify for a maker
repeatedly inspecting their own image over many rounds — change blindness under
repeated exposure, expertise/schema-driven viewing, perceptual/semantic satiation —
and after roughly how many exposures does defect detection measurably drop? Give
effect sizes with citations.

2. SATISFACTION OF SEARCH: by how much does finding one defect suppress detection
of remaining defects in the same image? Give the radiology numbers with citations,
and name the interventions (checklist-driven second read, forced re-scan of cleared
zones, structured reporting) that measurably restore second-defect detection, with
their measured effect.

3. What controlled evidence exists that viewing an image INVERTED, MIRROR-FLIPPED,
BLURRED or at CHANGED SCALE restores error detection for a habituated viewer, and
which transformation shows the largest measured effect? This is the countermeasure
we have never tried — please be thorough and cite specific studies, including any
from proofreading, art practice, radiology, or industrial inspection.

4. How much time away, or how much context change, does a creator need before
defect detection recovers toward fresh-viewer levels? Is there evidence that breaks
of about an hour do NOT restore it, and any measured decay curve?

5. In psychophysics, how much more sensitive is two-alternative forced choice (or
paired ranking) than single-stimulus absolute rating for small quality differences?
Give the sensitivity relationship with citations, and say whether side-by-side vs
sequential presentation changes detection rates.

6. In high-volume visual inspection QA, do enforced systematic scan patterns or
per-zone checklists measurably reduce misses in frequently-viewed regions versus
free viewing — and at what false-alarm and time cost?

7. What is the measured incremental defect-catch rate of a SECOND INDEPENDENT
READER (radiology double reading, print proofing, VFX dailies), and what reviewer-
rotation cadence or independence structure does the evidence support? If the
literature gives a number for how many reviewers before returns diminish, give it.

8. Does naming or labelling an object before inspection measurably reduce detection
of its FORM errors, and is withholding the label from the inspector a validated
countermeasure? Related: does the low-prevalence effect (rare defects missed more
often) apply when a reviewer expects most items to be correct, and what is its
measured size?
