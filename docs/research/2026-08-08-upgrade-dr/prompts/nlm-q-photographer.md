This notebook holds design-systems sources including architectural photography and
CG-lighting authorities (Shulman, Birn). Answer from those, with a source title per
number. If a question is not covered, write NOT IN SOURCES.

1. For a hero photograph of an interior, which lighting layers do the sources say
the photographer switches ON, OFF or dims, and for what stated reason?

2. What exposure approach do the sources describe — single exposure, bracketed HDR,
or flash-plus-ambient blending — and what luminance ratio between a visible lamp
glow and the surrounding wall do they target so the lamp reads as ON without
clipping? Give stop values if the sources state them.

3. What do the sources say about the LIGHT STORY of an interior image — the
deliberate hierarchy of bright and dark zones, where the eye is meant to land, and
how the photographer or lighting artist constructs it? This is the question I care
most about: give the sources' own vocabulary and any numbers they attach to it.

4. What do the sources say about the ratio of the brightest to the darkest
meaningful surface in a well-lit interior photograph, and about how much of the
frame should be in shadow?

5. On CAMERA: what focal lengths and camera heights do the sources give for
interiors, per room type or room size, and what do they say about vertical shift /
keeping verticals parallel? Attribute per source — do not merge different sources
into one range.

6. What do the sources say separates an amateur interior photograph from a
professional one, in terms that could be measured from the image itself?

7. Do any sources describe how a rendered interior is judged against a photographed
one — what is compared, in what order?
