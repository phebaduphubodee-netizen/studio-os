CONTEXT: A rendered interior is failing a craft critique on JUNCTIONS: the walls
meet the floor as a single sharp line with no skirting, no shadow gap and no
scribe; door frames run straight into the parquet; wardrobe panel joints have no
declared setting-out rule. These are the details a builder cannot avoid and a
renderer can accidentally omit. I need sourced dimensions and the rules that
govern them, for contemporary Asian/Thai and international residential work.

QUESTIONS:

1. WALL-TO-FLOOR. What are the actual options at a wall-to-floor junction in
contemporary residential interiors — timber/MDF skirting, flush skirting, negative
detail / shadow gap, scribed reveal, no detail at all — and what dimensions does
each carry (skirting height and projection in mm, shadow-gap width and depth in mm)?
Cite manufacturer systems for shadow-gap/flush-skirting profiles by name and give
their published dimensions. Which is dominant in contemporary minimal interiors,
and is there any source for "no detail at all" being buildable?

2. Is a shadow gap detail visible in a photograph taken from about 2–4 m — what
does it look like tonally (how dark, how wide in pixels at typical framing), and
what makes it read as a gap rather than as a dark line drawn on a wall?

3. DOOR AND OPENING. What are the standard reveal/architrave options at a door
opening in the same register — architrave, shadow-gap frameless, plaster return —
and the dimensions of each? What happens at the threshold where the frame meets a
timber floor?

4. JOINERY SETTING-OUT. What do joinery quality standards (AWI/WI/AWMAC Architectural
Woodwork Standards, or European equivalents) require about the setting-out of
panel/door widths in a wardrobe run — are equal bays a requirement, a convention,
or neither? What do the standards say about aligning joints with adjacent elements
(bulkheads, ledges, adjacent runs), and what reveal/gap width between door leaves
is specified?

5. HANDLE-LESS DETAILS. For handle-less wardrobe and kitchen fronts, what are the
published dimensions of the common systems — finger pull / J-pull profile depth and
height, push-to-open gap width, rail-integrated grip? At what viewing distance does
each stop being visible?

6. CEILING JUNCTION. What are the options and dimensions where a wall meets a
ceiling in the same contemporary register — cornice, shadow gap, plain butt — and
what shadow-gap dimensions do the manufacturer systems publish?

7. TOLERANCE. What construction tolerances do standards allow at these junctions
(gap variation along a run, plumb and level deviation over a wall's length), and is
there published guidance on how a *rendered* interior should treat tolerance —
perfectly aligned, or deliberately imperfect?

8. Is there published analysis of which junction details, when omitted, most damage
the realism of an architectural render? If it exists only as practitioner opinion,
say so and give the strongest sources.
