CONTEXT: A studio must author interior camera positions for rooms where no
reference photograph exists. Its current constants — 1.15 m eye height, a fixed
vertical shift — rest on one data point and on nothing at all respectively. It
keeps lens-band claims in a ledger that forbids merging separate sources into false
corroboration, so per-source numbers are more useful than an averaged consensus.

QUESTIONS:

1. What focal lengths (35 mm equivalent) do professional interior photographers and
archviz artists actually use per room type and room size — small bedroom, master
bedroom, living room, kitchen, bathroom? Give per-source numbers with attribution,
not a blended range.

2. What camera height above finished floor do interior photographers use, and how
does it change by room type (bathroom, kitchen, bedroom, living)? Is the often-
quoted 1.2–1.5 m band sourced, and what do practitioners say about shooting lower
for rooms whose subject is furniture?

3. What are the stated conventions for vertical shift / horizon placement — keeping
verticals parallel, where the horizon line falls in the frame, and how much rise or
fall is applied with a tilt-shift lens or its digital equivalent?

4. One-point vs two-point perspective: what selection criteria do published guides
give for interiors — when a straight-on one-point view is chosen and when a corner
two-point view is chosen — and does any source quantify the yaw angle band that
separates them?

5. What differs between the LISTING/marketing register and the EDITORIAL/portfolio
register in interior photography — framing, lens, height, how much of the room is
included, straight-on vs angled?

6. What orientation and aspect ratio do professionals use per room type, and what
do published sources say about the influence of the target medium (print, MLS
listing, Instagram) on that choice?

7. What quantitative signatures separate amateur from professional interior images —
convergence of verticals in degrees, barrel distortion, how much of the ceiling and
floor is in frame, subject placement? Anything measurable enough that it could be
computed from an image and used as an authoring-side check.

8. Is there any published dataset or analysis that MEASURES camera parameters
across a corpus of professional interior photographs (focal length distribution,
height distribution, shift), rather than prescribing them? Name it if it exists.
