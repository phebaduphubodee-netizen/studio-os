### 1. LAYERS AND ORDER

*   **Assembly Sequence & Named Layers (bottom to top)**:
    *   According to **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"**, a professionally made bed is configured as follows:
        *   **Layer 1 (Mattress Protector / Pad)**: Placed over the mattress and fully concealed under the bottom sheet [1, 2].
        *   **Layer 2 (First Bed Sheet - Fitted or Flat)**: Fits snugly over the mattress [1, 2].
        *   **Layer 3 (Second Bed Sheet - Flat)**: Laid face-down (wrong-side up); top edge aligns with the mattress head [1, 2].
        *   **Layer 4 (Warmth Layer - Blanket, Quilt, or Duvet)**: Positioned **6 inches (15.24 cm)** lower than the second sheet or below the mattress top edge [1, 2].
        *   **Layer 5 (Third Bed Sheet - Flat - Triple Sheeting Only)**: Spread over the blanket layer and folded back with the second sheet [1, 2].
        *   **Layer 6 (Bedspread / Comforter Cover)**: Standard comforters hang over the sides by **40 cm (400 mm)** or have a standard **13-inch (33.02 cm)** queen overhang [1, 2].
        *   **Layer 7 (Decorative Bed Runner / Throw)**: Spans **two-thirds to three-quarters** of the bed's width, placed across the bottom third of the mattress [1, 2].
    *   In **"Double and Triple Sheeting Guide | Hotel Bedding Look Without a Duvet Cover"**, the order is fitted sheet, flat sheet, lightweight blanket or duvet insert, and a second flat sheet on top (optionally finished with a decorative bed scarf/third sheet at the foot) [3, 4].
    *   In **"Staging a Bed (Expert Tips for 2026)"**, a residential staging layout sequence layers a bed skirt, fitted sheet, flat sheet (face-down), bedspread/blanket (leaving **1 foot** of space at the head), a comforter folded in thirds across the bottom of the bed, pillows, and a throw/shawl across the foot [5].

*   **Exposure and Band Depths**:
    *   Except for the specified sheet folds and drops detailed below, specific exposed band depths for the remaining layers are **NOT IN SOURCES** [1, 2].

### 2. DUVET FOLD-BACK

*   **Fold-back Band Depth (Measurements)**:
    *   **12 inches (30.48 cm)** from the headboard for flat sheets folded back over the duvet (**"Hotel Bedding Layers Explained: How to Build the Perfect Luxury Bed"** [6] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [7, 8]).
    *   **6 to 8 inches (15.24 to 203.2 mm)** visible cuff of the top sheet and blanket folded back together in traditional commercial bed-making (**"Hotel Housekeeping Bed Making Procedure | SOP | 2025"** [9] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [10, 11]).
    *   **15 cm (150 mm)** double-fold folding sequence in triple-sheeted environments, where the sheets and blanket are folded back together to encapsulate the blanket edge (**"Triple Sheeting Method - How Hotels Make Beds with 3 Sheets - 1Concier"** [12] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [10, 11]).
    *   **1 foot (30.48 cm)** space left between the top edge of the blanket and the headboard, over which the flat sheet is folded back (**"Staging a Bed (Expert Tips for 2026)"** [5] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [13, 14]).
    *   **1.5 feet (45.72 cm)** down fold for both the blanket and comforter from the top of the bed (**"Hotel Bed Making Procedure Guide"** and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [13, 14]).
    *   **18 inches (45.72 cm)** lower than the mattress edge for standard blanket spread, and **1 foot (30.48 cm)** downward for a duvet cover and in-fill fold (**"HK-LAS-Week-5"** and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [13, 14]).
    *   **Exactly one-quarter (1/4)** of the bed length pulled down from the top (**"bedmaking-procedure"** and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [13, 14]).

*   **Staging Style Quantities / Ratios**:
    *   The exact share or percentage of professional bedroom photography where the duvet is laid flat to the headboard or tucked under the pillow rank without any fold-back is **NOT IN SOURCES** [15, 16].
    *   The exact duvet fold-back depth expressed as a fraction of total bed length is **NOT IN SOURCES** [15, 16].

*   **Hotel Turndown vs. Daytime-Made Styling**:
    *   **Daytime-Made Styling**: Highly structured; the bedspread is smoothed over the pillows to the headboard, and the remaining fabric is tucked under the front edge of the pillows to present a firm, unbroken plane (**"Hotel Housekeeping Bed Making Procedure | SOP | 2025"** [9] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [15, 16]).
    *   **Evening Turndown Styling**: Designed for guest accessibility; the bed is prepared by peeling back the top layers at a **45-degree angle** on the side nearest the nightstand, positioning slippers, setting bedside lighting, and removing room-service trays (**"Hotel Housekeeping SOPs: Room Cleaning, Turndown & Public Area Procedures (2026)"** [17, 18], **"Housekeeping SOP: Hotel Room Cleaning, Inspection & HSK Procedures | LuxOps"** [19], and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [20, 21]).

### 3. SIDE DROP

*   **Drop Lengths by Fabric Class (Mattress Top to Lower Edge)**:
    *   **Coverlet**: **8 to 12 inches (203.2 to 304.8 mm)** (**"Quilt Sizes Chart: Standard Dimensions for Every Quilt Size - Stitchin' Heaven"** [22] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [23, 24]).
    *   **Comforter**: **12 to 16 inches (304.8 to 406.4 mm)** (**"Quilt Sizes Chart: Standard Dimensions for Every Quilt Size - Stitchin' Heaven"** [22] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [23, 24]).
    *   **Bedspread**: **18 to 25 inches (457.2 to 635.0 mm)** (**"Quilt Sizes Chart: Standard Dimensions for Every Quilt Size - Stitchin' Heaven"** [22] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [23, 24]).
    *   **Bed Skirt / Valance**: **14 inches (355.6 mm) or 15 inches (381.0 mm)** (**"Bedroom Staging Checklist: How to Stage a Bedroom to Sell - create design home"** [25], **"What Is a Bed Skirt? Sizes + the Best Alternatives (2026) | Or & Zon"** [26], and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [23, 24]).
    *   **Bed Throw / Runner**: **30 to 45 cm (300 to 450 mm)** (**"Throw Blanket Size Guide: What Size Do You Actually Need? - Heating & Plumbing London"** [27] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [23, 24]).

*   **Mattress Drop Targets**:
    *   Standard innerspring mattresses (**8 to 10 inches** thick) target a drop of **10 to 12 inches (254.0 to 304.8 mm)** (**"Standard Quilt Sizes Chart — Baby to King, Drops & Overhangs - QuiltKeeper Studio"** [28] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [29, 30]).
    *   Pillow-top mattresses (**12 to 16 inches** thick) require a drop of **14 to 18 inches (355.6 to 457.2 mm)** (**"Standard Quilt Sizes Chart — Baby to King, Drops & Overhangs - QuiltKeeper Studio"** [28] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [29, 30]).
    *   Memory foam mattresses (**10 to 12 inches** thick) require a drop of **12 to 14 inches (304.8 to 355.6 mm)** (**"Standard Quilt Sizes Chart — Baby to King, Drops & Overhangs - QuiltKeeper Studio"** [28] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [29, 30]).
    *   Platform beds (**8 to 10 inches** thick) require an **8 to 12 inch (203.2 to 304.8 mm)** drop (**"Standard Quilt Sizes Chart — Baby to King, Drops & Overhangs - QuiltKeeper Studio"** [28] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [29, 30]).
    *   Old-style box spring and mattress setups (**18 to 24 inches** thick) require a drop of **18 to 24 inches (457.2 to 609.6 mm)** (**"Standard Quilt Sizes Chart — Baby to King, Drops & Overhangs - QuiltKeeper Studio"** [28] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [29, 30]).
    *   **Styling Default Standard**: **16 to 18 inches (406.4 to 457.2 mm)** of side drop and **15 to 16 inches (381.0 to 406.4 mm)** of drop at the foot (**"Standard Quilt Sizes Chart — Baby to King, Drops & Overhangs - QuiltKeeper Studio"** [28] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [29, 30]).
    *   The precise drop bands in millimeters directly mapped to a mattress height of ~200-350 mm are **NOT IN SOURCES** [29, 30].

*   **Acceptability of Bare Mattress Visibility**:
    *   Exposed mattress sides are **never acceptable** in professional staging or hospitality photography; they must be concealed using bed skirts, wraps, or oversized, low-draping duvets and coverlets (**"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [31, 32]).

### 4. PILLOW STAGING

*   **Pillow Arrangements (Ranks and Counts)**:
    *   According to **"Styling Your Bed is Easy with Our Pillow Formations Chart | Matouk Luxury Linens"** [33, 34] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [35, 36]:
        *   **Queen (Four-Layer Setup)**:
            *   Rank 1 (Back): Two Euro shams (**27" x 27"** with a **1" flange**).
            *   Rank 2: Two standard sleeping pillows.
            *   Rank 3: Two standard shams (**21" x 27"** with a **1" flange**).
            *   Rank 4 (Front): Two boudoir pillows (**12" x 16"**).
        *   **Queen (Propped Headboard Setup)**:
            *   Rank 1: Sleeping pillows propped upright against the headboard.
            *   Rank 2: Standard shams layered in front.
            *   Rank 3: One centered boudoir pillow (**12" x 16"**).
        *   **Queen (Streamlined Setup)**:
            *   Rank 1: Two Euro shams pulled slightly to outer edges.
            *   Rank 2: Two boudoir pillows pulled slightly to center.
        *   **King (The Layered Trio)**:
            *   Rank 1: Three Euro shams (**27" x 27"**).
            *   Rank 2: Two King-size pillows (**50 x 90 cm / 20" x 36"**) in decorative shams.
            *   Rank 3: Three boudoir shams (**12" x 16"**).
        *   **King (Symmetrical Setup)**:
            *   Rank 1: Three Euro shams.
            *   Rank 2: Two King pillows in King shams.
            *   Rank 3: Two decorative pillows (**20" x 20"**) centered.
        *   **King (Gap-Filling Setup)**:
            *   Rank 1: Three Euro shams.
            *   Rank 2: Three standard or queen shams.
            *   Rank 3: Two boudoir pillows centered over the gaps of the middle rank.
    *   **Standard Pillow Counts**: 5-star properties utilize **4 to 6 pillows** (mixing sleeping and decorative), whereas 3-star properties utilize only **2 synthetic pillows** (**"Hotel Bedding Layers Explained: How to Build the Perfect Luxury Bed"** [37] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [38, 39]).

*   **Lean Angle, Neighbor Overlap Distance, and Rear Rank Visibility**:
    *   The precise lean angle in degrees, horizontal overlap in millimeters, and the exact height of each rear rank that stays visible are **NOT IN SOURCES** [38, 39]. (Note: Rectangular front pillows should be aligned and offset slightly from the outer edges of the rear Euro shams [38, 39]).

*   **The "Karate Chop" Cushion Debate**:
    *   **The Case For**: Applying a sharp downward blow to feather-filled pillows catches localized highlights, highlights fabric texture, and signals a freshly fluffed/cleaned surface (**"Karate-Chop and 3 Other Weird Things our Stylists Do"** [40], **"The Karate Pillow Chop: An Exploration"** [41], and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [42, 43]). To hold the shape, stylists use inserts sized **1 inch larger** than the cover (**"Primary Bedroom Staging Guide: Create Your Luxury Retreat for Buyers | Olive Branch Staging"** [42, 43]).
    *   **The Case Against**: Designers like Jan Showers call the chop a dated **"1982"** aesthetic that breaks bedding's natural drape (**"The Karate Pillow Chop: An Exploration"** [44] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [42, 43]). Photographers argue it looks highly contrived, artificial, and disheveled on patterned cushions (**"Don't 'karate chop' my pillows! | Houzz Forum"** [45] and **"Professional Bed Styling Standards and Photographic Appearance Targets for CG Artists"** [42, 43]).
