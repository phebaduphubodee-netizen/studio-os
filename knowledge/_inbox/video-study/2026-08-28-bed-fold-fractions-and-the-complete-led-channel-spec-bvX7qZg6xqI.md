## 1. THE BED, WITH FRACTIONS

Studio McGee (`QF8hplMHZ2Y`) gave the layer STACK and the structured-versus-relaxed law but
no quantities. This source gives the quantities, for three different finished looks built on
the same bed.

### 1.1 THE FOLD-BACK IS A THIRD
`[02:25]` *"after tucked and straightened, you'll want to **pull back the sheet along with a
coverlet about A THIRD OF THE WAY DOWN**. The sheet will naturally extend further, which is
fine, because the next step is to **take the excess and fold it TOWARDS THE HEADBOARD**."*

Then `[03:01]` *"I'm going to take you one step further and **bring that blanket up before
your final fold**."*

**So the fold-back band sits at ~1/3 of the bed length from the head**, and the sheet's cuff
turns back OVER the coverlet — which is what produces the layered band Studio McGee calls
*"an extra fluffy band across the bed."*

### 1.2 THE FOLDED QUILT COVERS THE LOWER HALF AND STOPS SHORT OF THE EDGE
`[03:01]` *"then we move on to the **quilt, which I fold in half** and place **evenly over the
lower half of the bed**. I'll **double it back and stop just before the edge of the
mattress**, making for a **great tailored look**."*

Two quantities: the folded quilt occupies **the lower half**, and its return **stops short of
the mattress edge** rather than overhanging it.

### 1.3 THE OTHER TWO LOOKS, ALSO WITH FRACTIONS
- `[03:36]` unfold the quilt and bring it up toward the head, *"stopping **a quarter of the
  way from the top**, make a fold, and now use that coverlet to go over the top."*
- `[04:47]` the duvet look: *"I **fold my duvet neatly in half** and place it evenly **at the
  foot of the bed**, then **one last fold**."*

### 1.4 TWO PREP STEPS THAT ARE ABOUT FOLD ENERGY
`[00:38]` new bedding is put **in the dryer** first *"to fluff and **get rid of the excess
creases**"*, and `[01:17]` the sewn-in tags are cut off.

> Consistent with the structured/relaxed law and with the hotel-photography floor: **creases
> are actively REMOVED before styling begins**, and only then is a controlled amount of
> softness put back. Our cloth arrives with its creases and never has them taken out.

### 1.5 PILLOWS ARE FACED AT THE DOOR
`[05:21]` *"for these I try to organise them in a way that's both **stylish and structural**.
I even **face the pillows in the direction of the door** — this creates a **great first
impression**."*

A staging rule keyed to the **entry**, not to the camera. Worth pairing with the
gaze-direction rule already recorded from two other sources: **objects are oriented toward
where a person arrives from.** Our frame has a camera but the spec has never named the door.

Also `[00:07]`: *"if you want to have a beautiful bedroom you need to start with the things
that **take centre stage** in your room, and that's the bed"*, and `[00:38]` the flat rejection
of the minimum — *"**just a comforter isn't gonna cut it either**."*

## 2. THE LED CHANNEL — the complete hardware spec, and a fourth "conceal it"

### 2.1 IT IS A CABINET-DESIGN DECISION, NOT A LIGHTING ONE
`[00:00]` *"**number one consideration at the beginning of a project, or during the cabinet
design**, that I always bring up is: **how are we going to mount the under-cabinet
lights?**"*

Two ways to hide it, and both are joinery:
- **A light rail** (in cabinet trade, a *style rail*) — *"about a **half inch or 3/4 inch
  lip**"* on the bottom edge of the carcass that the channel hides behind.
- **A dado** — *"if it's a more modern design, oftentimes the channel profiles… can be
  **dadoed into the bottom of the cabinet so that they end up sitting DEAD FLUSH**."*

### 2.2 THE FOURTH INDEPENDENT "DO NOT SEE THE SOURCE"
`[00:46]` *"**I don't want to see the LED.** This is supposed to be an **accent light**. It's
supposed to be **out of sight, out of mind**, and just light the tops of the counters… **A pet
peeve of mine is being able to see the lens. We don't want to see where the light's coming
from.**"*

After John Cullen (*"concealing light is what a lighting designer spends a lot of their time
doing"*), the routed-dado carpenter, and the Thai designer's concealed headboard uplight,
this is the **fourth** independent professional saying the same thing. C2's item on our
casework — *"they glow but light nothing"* — is now the most-corroborated defect in the whole
study.

### 2.3 THE PROFILES, WITH DIMENSIONS
| Profile | Size | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **½ in × ½ in** | 12.7 × 12.7 mm | *"most of the time we're using"* it; **square edges** so a cabinetmaker can dado it from a sample piece |
| **¼ in** | 6.4 mm | *"if you're working with like a tile detail or something that you can't get a half-inch profile in"* — check the tape is compatible with the width and depth |
| **45° corner** | — | *"if we wanted to mount it in a corner and **shoot the light out at a 45° angle**"* — used *"on the backs of rails in a cabinet to **light bookshelves**"* |

And a placement number for the angled case `[03:42]`: *"we mounted this **3½ inches behind
the face**… that's a pretty **industry standard**. It gives us a really good cast of light."*
→ **89 mm setback.**

### 2.4 THE REST OF THE ASSEMBLY
- The channel is *"an **aluminium empty profile with an opaque lens** that the LED tape hides
  behind, and this **opaque lens diffuses the light**"* — available **clear or frosted**.
- Sold in **6 ft and 8 ft** lengths, cuttable on a band saw or chop saw, and *"you can **butt
  them up against each other**"* for long runs.
- Wiring: *"we'd have to get **one wire, ideally on the top**, we'd **drop it down at the end
  point** of where you're going to run it — that gives you a good starting point."* Then
  *"the profile goes in, terminate your low-voltage wire to the end of the LED tape, sit it
  in there, **put the lens on**."*
- The same detail is used *"in **stair treads**"* and *"in **cove light valances**"* — which is
  exactly the Thai designer's concealed headboard uplight and hidden handrail LED.

> **P2r-29(b) now has a complete, unambiguous specification**: a ½″ × ½″ aluminium channel
> either dadoed flush into the underside or hidden behind a ½–¾″ light rail, carrying a
> **frosted lens**, with the tape invisible and a cable dropped at one end. A 45° channel set
> **89 mm behind the face** if the light must be thrown rather than dropped. Nothing in that
> list is a shader value.

## 3. WHAT IS OWED FROM THIS UNIT

1. **Bed fractions**: fold-back at ~1/3 from the head with the sheet cuff turned back over
   the coverlet; folded quilt over the lower half stopping short of the mattress edge.
   These are buildable box-and-fold geometry, not simulation.
2. **Remove creases before adding softness** (§1.4) — the prep step our cloth never gets.
3. **Face the pillows toward the door** (§1.5), and name the door in the spec.
4. **Build the LED as a ½″ channel + frosted lens**, dadoed flush or behind a ½–¾″ rail, with
   a cable route (§2) — and record that concealing the source is now a four-source rule.
