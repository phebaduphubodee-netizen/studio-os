## 1. FULLNESS — the number stack-back depends on

`[07:52]` *"**fullness** really relates to the feel of the drapery. So if you think about
draperies that are really traditional, and they bell out at the bottom and they're very full
and super lush — that's a **high fullness level, probably 300 %**. Now if you think of
something more contemporary, like this **ripple fold** drapery that just **stacks back into
a narrow, slender stack** at the side, that's gonna be a smaller fullness percentage, so
perhaps like **150 %**."*

**VG-08** records that stack-back per metre by heading type and fullness is a track
manufacturer's table. This gives the input to that table:

| Style | Fullness | Stack |
|---|---|---|
| traditional, lush, belled | **300 %** | wide |
| contemporary ripple fold | **150 %** | *"narrow, slender stack"* |

And it names the heading that belongs with our condition: **ripple fold on a ceiling
track**, `[04:51]` *"if you're all the way on the contemporary side of things, your hardware
might be something as **invisible as a simple ripplefold track that's mounted in the
ceiling** and you don't see anything else."*

> Our sheers are floor-to-ceiling on a ceiling line with no visible hardware. That is the
> contemporary case, which means **~150 % fullness and a ripple-fold heading** — a specific,
> buildable pleat geometry rather than the unnamed gathers we have now.

## 2. TWO RULES OF THUMB, BOTH DIMENSIONED

`[09:41]`:
1. *"you always **hang your drapery as close to the ceiling as you can**, within, you know,
   **three or four inches**, something along those lines. Now if you have deep crown
   moulding, obviously you're hanging it from the **bottom of the crown moulding** — you're
   not covering your crown moulding."* → **76-102 mm below the ceiling**, or below the
   cornice if there is one.
2. *"my preference for hem is that drapery **graze the floor**, or if you're in a condition
   where it's appropriate, you can allow them to **puddle** a little bit — they look very
   elegant that way but they are a bit of a maintenance issue. Then just hem them right at
   say **an eighth of an inch or a quarter of an inch above the floor**, or say the **carpet
   top** if you're dealing with carpets."* → **3.2-6.4 mm clear**, measured to the carpet
   surface where there is one.

And the failure mode, stated as the thing to avoid above all others:
> `[10:16]` *"**if they are too short, drapery tend to look like they have been shrunk. And
> that's the thing you want to avoid at all costs.**"*

Three numbers our sheers have never been checked against: top gap, hem gap, and hem gap
measured **to the rug surface** rather than the floor — which matters in our room because
the rug is under the window wall.

## 3. THE REST OF THE VOCABULARY, so the spec can name what it builds

- **Five factors before choosing anything** `[00:38]`: architectural style of the house;
  privacy and light control (*"bedrooms are different than living rooms"*); special
  conditions (baseboard heaters, radiators, an HOA requiring uniformity from outside);
  window type and shape; and which of the five treatment families — **drapery, shades,
  roller shades, blinds, shutters**.
- **Drapery defined** `[02:24]`: *"fabric panels that hang on either side of a window or a
  door **and they can be pulled across** to cover the space"* — as against **fixed panels**,
  *"they don't move across, they just act as a decorative element."* Ours have never been
  declared as one or the other, and a fixed panel is a different object from a working one.
- **Lining is a separate selection** `[03:37]`: blackout / light-blocking for bedrooms and
  nurseries, privacy lining, thermal lining. A bedroom sheer with no lining is a choice that
  should be stated.
- **Top styles** `[06:39]`: tab top, single / double / **triple pleat** (*"really add a lot
  of fullness"*), **inverted or box pleat** (*"super tailored and kind of simple but it does
  give a little bit of fullness"*), grommet, ring-with-tab, plain/rod top, pocket or
  gathered top.
- **Hardware is jewellery** `[04:14]`: rods, **double rods** (*"so that you can hang two
  types of drapery, a sheer and an over-drapery"*), **traverse rods** for long spans
  (*"saves on the wear and tear"*), rings, hooks, **finials**, brackets, tiebacks — and
  **pull buttons** `[06:03]`: *"if you have a big window you're definitely gonna want this,
  'cause you're not gonna be wanting to **pull the drapery from the front end** — that will
  actually **pull it out of line and out of shape** eventually."*
- **Valances, cornices, swags and jabots** `[08:28]` belong to traditional and transitional
  work; contemporary is *"a lack of pleats altogether… plain hardware or maybe **no hardware
  at all, just the ceiling mount**."*

## 4. THE REALISM UNIT — useful framing, and a disclosure

`0OVEJVbklV0` opens with a framing worth keeping `[00:44]`: the three forces that decide
whether an image reads as real are **the subject, the lighting, and the camera** — and the
example is an animation of *"only simple models in a simple scene"* that was removed from
r/blender for being judged real footage. *"The combination of model quality, environment,
natural lighting and a hero role for the camera work had many believe that this was real."*

**That is the same three-part split this repo's own lanes are organised around** (objects,
light, camera), arrived at independently, and it argues that our frame's problem is unlikely
to be any single one of them.

**The disclosure: the rest of the video is a sponsored 3D-scanning tutorial.** He says so
`[02:49]`: *"my personal 3D scanning app of choice is Kiri, who — to be fully transparent —
also sponsored this video."* Its content is photogrammetry technique, not general realism.

That is still worth one line for us, because **it is a third acquisition route R8 has never
named**: build, acquire — or **scan**. `[02:07]` *"we can take a huge shortcut in creating
realistic models by using the power of **3D scanning** — this technique will let you capture
**minute details, imperfections, textures** and the most complex geometry you can think of
in a matter of minutes… all you really need is a **phone**."* For a project whose client and
site are both in Thailand and whose owner already photographs everything, scanning a real
object is a genuinely available path to *"imperfections"* that neither building nor buying
gives us.

Its scanning conditions, recorded because they are the part that generalises `[04:57]`:
soft, single-colour, **even** light; never direct sun; **overcast is "nature's softbox"**;
objects need feature points, and featureless ones scan badly; overlap every frame.

## 5. WHAT IS OWED FROM THIS UNIT

1. **Declare the sheers' fullness (~150 %) and heading (ripple fold)** and build that pleat
   geometry, rather than unnamed gathers.
2. **Check three gaps**: top 76-102 mm below the ceiling line, hem 3-6 mm clear, and hem
   measured **to the rug top** where the rug passes under. *"Too short"* is the named
   cardinal failure.
3. **Declare whether our panels are working or FIXED** — they are different objects.
4. **Declare the lining** — a bedroom sheer with no lining is a choice, not a default.
5. Add **scan** to R8's build-or-acquire fork as a third branch, with the caveat that this
   particular source is sponsored and the technique should be checked elsewhere before any
   spend.
