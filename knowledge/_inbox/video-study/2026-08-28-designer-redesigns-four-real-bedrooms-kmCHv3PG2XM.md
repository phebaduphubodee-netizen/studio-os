## 1. THE READ-ORDER — the deliverable of this video

He is handed printed photographs of four real bedrooms and works each one in the same
sequence. He never begins with decoration.

1. **Whole-room verdict first, in two or three words.** `[00:15]` *"These are all very
   real spaces… there's quite a bit of clutter."* `[08:08]` *"it's just really, really
   boring."* `[08:53]` *"What she needs to do is to soften up the room. Everything is very
   hard."*
2. **LAYOUT / orientation** — before a single object is chosen. Three of the four rooms
   get the bed moved: `[01:07]` *"What I would tell Amy is to reorientate the bed. Right
   now it takes up pretty much the whole room. You walk in and you sort of have like a
   hallway that leads to kind of nowhere."*
3. **THE BASICS, explicitly before the fun** — `[05:23]` *"Attack the basics first before
   we really get into the fun things."* The basics he names are cords, storage and mess.
4. **A FOCAL POINT, named as a required part.** `[05:47]` *"I think what I really want to
   do is create a focal point."* `[09:11]` *"Clearly, her room needs a sort of focal
   point."* Every room gets exactly one, and it is always an architectural feature or a
   made-up wall moment — the arched window plus wooden beam, a draped headboard wall, a
   canopy.
5. **Soft goods to soften** — curtains, rug, upholstery.
6. **Colour last**, and derived from something already in the room: `[07:33]` *"I really
   like the idea of picking up on the color of the beam."*

His closing summary of the whole exercise, `[10:33]`: *"My main two pieces of advice for
your bedroom, consider softness. You want it to be completely soft inside and you also
want it to be very well lit."*

> **For our lane.** This is a read-order the builder may run over its own render before a
> critic sees it — as a REPORT, never as a conviction (R7d: C1 aims, it does not judge).
> Note what is *not* in the list: not one of the six steps is a material parameter, and
> the first three are all things our gate cannot currently see at all.

## 2. FOUR ITEMS THAT LAND DIRECTLY ON OPEN WORK ITEMS

### 2.1 CORDS — a designer's *first* basic, and our frame has none at all

`[05:26]` *"All of these cords are, they're a nightmare, okay? If you have a lamp that's
on top of your dresser, you just gotta tuck that cord behind the dresser. You don't have
to have 'em dangling out like this. If you have a bedside table that's next to your bed,
why don't we have all of those cords come from behind it."*

And the deliberate opposite, twice, as a *design element* rather than an accident:
`[02:42]` *"putting the sconces on the wall with a cord behind and it just sort of peeks
out through the curtain"*; `[09:26]` *"Maybe some sconces are on some wires and you mount
through the wall and they can actually peek out through the curtain."*

**Bearing on P2r-29(c).** "Lamps with no cords" is on the SENSE list as a critic item the
builder has not yet built. This video is independent evidence that it is not a pedantic
item: a working designer opens the *basics* section of a bedroom critique with cords, in
three of four rooms. It also supplies the grammar for the fix, which is not "add a wire":
a cord either **runs behind its own furniture and is not seen**, or it is **deliberately
exposed against the wall as part of the fixture**. There is no third state, and our lamps
currently have neither.

### 2.2 THE AIRCON UNIT — the answer to "no aircon anywhere in the room"

`[09:33]` *"Maya has this kind of a AC unit on the wall, they're never fun to work with.
What I would first suggest to her is to, we can kind of build out a sort of box around
that to disguise it, paint it the same color as everything else."*

**Bearing on P2r-29(c).** Our room has no aircon, no door, no socket, no switch. The
critics filed the absence; this video shows what the presence looks like in a designed
room — and that the designed answer is a **boxed-in bulkhead painted out**, not a bare
grille. For a Thai master suite the unit is not optional in reality, so the honest choice
is a painted-out box or a declared gap (R10), never silence.

### 2.3 CURTAINS — track, ceiling, wall-to-wall, and used to hide clothes

`[03:56]` *"That curtain's gonna run wall to wall. It can be on a track that's mounted on
top of the ceiling."*
`[09:14]` *"She could hang curtains above this full wall right here. That would come from
the ceiling, that would go behind her headboard and it could also start to disguise this
door that's off center."*

**Bearing on P2r-29 / P2r-30.** Our curtain tops vanish into the ceiling slab with no
pocket and no track — filed by all three critics, invisible to `carry_check` because the
rung reads it as FIXED-IN. The designer states the real-world member by name: a **track
mounted on the ceiling**, running wall to wall, deliberately *wider than the window*. The
fix is therefore not to shorten the curtain; it is to build the member it hangs from.

And, unprompted, the same designer's answer to a room with too many visible clothes:
`[03:46]` *"have a zone right here for all of her clothing… I think Cynthia needs another
rack for her clothes. What we're gonna do then is disguise it all behind a curtain."*

**Bearing on the standing owner verdict about built-in contents (ORD-2026-08-26).** One
legitimate designer answer to "hanging garments read wrong" is that in a designed room
they are often **not on show at all**. That is a design option our lane has never
considered — it has only ever tried to make the exposed rail look better. It is not a
recommendation, it is a second branch that costs nothing to evaluate.

### 2.4 THE RUG — a rule stated as a defect

`[07:11]` *"Your rug is too big, okay? It hits the baseboard."* and the alternative
`[07:17]` *"you go almost wall to wall with a carpet. You can do it in a solid color, and
what it does, it really makes the space feel one very, very soft… and two, what it can do
is to tie everything together."*

**Bearing on ORD-2026-08-25b ("พรมยังไม่สวย").** Two thresholds in one line: a rug must
**not touch the skirting**, and the escape hatch when the room is small is to go *almost*
wall-to-wall in a solid colour. Both are testable against our frame from the scene dump
without a render.

## 3. THE VOCABULARY — what a designer's sentence looks like

Collected because the builder's failure mode is that it can measure a defect but cannot
*name* one. Every phrase below is a whole-room judgement made in under five words, with
the room visible.

| Phrase | Timestamp | What it is judging |
|---|---|---|
| *"quite a bit of clutter"* | `[00:23]` | object count / surface discipline |
| *"it feels like a closet"* | `[01:06]` | circulation, not decoration |
| *"a hallway that leads to kind of nowhere"* | `[01:14]` | leftover floor with no purpose |
| *"it's just really, really boring"* | `[08:08]` | no focal point, no contrast |
| *"Everything is very hard"* / *"very rigid"* | `[08:53]` `[09:01]` | absence of soft goods |
| *"they're a nightmare"* (cords) | `[05:26]` | services left visible |
| *"your rug is too big"* | `[07:11]` | one measurable object rule |
| *"needs a sort of focal point"* | `[09:11]` | the room has no subject |

> Three of these — *hard / rigid*, *boring*, *no focal point* — are the exact register in
> which our own frame has been judged by C2, C3 and the owner, and none of them is a
> material setting. That is the same lesson R7b bought with the 0.65× figure: **the eye
> names the class, the instrument measures the amount.**

## 4. WHAT THIS VIDEO IS NOT, said plainly

- It is **remote consultation on photographs**, so nothing is ever built or photographed
  afterwards. The *diagnosis* half is the value; the *prescription* half ends at a marker
  sketch and is never tested.
- Every room is a small New York rental. Not one number is stated anywhere in eleven
  minutes — no rug clearance in mm, no curtain drop, no cord routing detail. **It supplies
  WHAT and WHY, and no HOW-MUCH at all**, which is exactly the split R7b names. Any
  quantity our lane needs must still be measured off the anchor pool.
- It says nothing about wardrobes, wood, texture, render realism or camera.
