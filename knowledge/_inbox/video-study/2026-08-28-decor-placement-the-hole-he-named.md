## 0. WHY THIS UNIT EXISTS, AND THE COUNT THAT MADE HIM RIGHT

He read the curriculum and said: *"ยังไม่เห็นมี video ที่เกี่ยวกับการจัดวางของตกแต่ง"* — no video
about the PLACEMENT of decor. Measured the same hour: **of 56 watched rows, 0 were about
arranging decorative objects on a surface.** The watched set held bed-MAKING, rug SIZING,
closet ORGANISING, visual merchandising, lighting and photography — every one of them about an
object class this studio already builds — and not one showed somebody choosing objects and
putting them down. The four rows in the ledger that WERE decor placement all sat at priority
`should`/`reference`, and the watch pass ran `must`-first.

**No counter in this repo could have found it.** G1 held 33 rows with 16 watched — the
best-covered G-item in the program. The hole was inside it. The G-items were written by the
builder from the builder's own defect list and the priorities were assigned by the same judges.
R7b one level up: an instrument answers the question it was built to ask.

**And the file it feeds says the same thing from the other side.** `decor-placement-grammar.md`
was distilled entirely from a text DR — §1 "Surface vignettes" carries counts, ratios and
tiers, and **not one video id**. The studio had a grammar for placing objects and had never
watched anybody place one.

## 1. WHAT WAS DONE

Two harvests, 533 candidates, **no URL ever written by a model** — every id came back from
`ytsearch` (the 2026-08-28 law, unchanged). Harvest 1 (31 queries, 216 ids) was scored by six
independent judge lanes and a completeness critic. **All six lanes reported the same failure and
it is worth keeping:** the harvest had run *"how to style a &lt;surface&gt;"*, which is the exact SEO
of the talking-head listicle, so it returned the genre the knowledge file was ALREADY built from.
The critic put it in one sentence — *"They are not a new source. They are the same source with a
face on it… Watching them will feel productive and change no pixel"* — and named the one question
that WAS new: **the relationship between an ARRANGEMENT and a LENS.** Harvest 2 (44 queries, 317
ids) was therefore run in the trade's own vocabulary — prop styling, set dressing, shoot day,
archviz dressing, Thai shelter publications, and queries naming a NUMBER instead of a surface.

Sources actually watched are listed per section below. Where a video returned little, it says so.

## 2. THE COFFEE-TABLE BUILD, WATCHED FRAME BY FRAME — `HXbnHgIr_C4`

Rebecca Robeson, *"The Accessorizing Formula That Makes Any Room Look Expensive"*, 22 min,
transcript + 60 extracted frames. She builds the same 36-inch (914 mm) round coffee table three
times, and the frames carry things the words do not.

**The tray is the ground plane, and it is CONDITIONAL on table size** `[00:47]`-`[01:01]`:
*"I start with a large tray and I keep everything in the tray… this is just 36 in around. It's a
small coffee table. If it's much larger than this, you can have some things on the tray and some
things on the table itself."*
> A rule the file states unconditionally now has its condition: **one bounded group on a small
> surface; a large surface may carry the tray group PLUS loose objects.** Our nightstands and
> bench are small surfaces and have no boundary object at all.

**The count is 7 on a SMALL round table, and she counts them on camera** `[03:26]`:
*"we have 1 2 3 4 5 6 7. Do you see how the odd numbers create a really beautiful and open, airy
look."*
> **This contradicts §1's surface map**, which assigns 3 to *"constrained surfaces"* and reserves
> 7 for *"expansive (long tables, multi-bay shelving)"*. A working designer puts seven on 914 mm.
> The file's mapping is [CITED] from two text sources; this is one practitioner performing it.
> Recorded as a live disagreement, not as a replacement — but our per-surface counts sit at the
> BOTTOM of both, which is the direction the "half-made" verdict points.

**Stacking to gain height — the book stack is a PLINTH, not an object** (frames 0088, 0055): two
coffee-table books lie flat and the coral sculpture sits ON them; a heavy bronze plate lies at
the very bottom and the pocket watch sits ON that. Even the smallest object gets its own ground
plane. `[02:12]` *"at the very bottom, I add this beautiful actual bronze plate… and on that, I
put my great-grandfather's pocket watch."*

**Cascade is defined against the VIEWER, not the object** `[10:48]`: *"the back one is the
tallest and then it comes down and the one that's in the front is the lowest. So, from your
vantage point, you've got this cascading effect back to front."*
> A camera-relative rule. Our one fixed hero camera makes this checkable and our vignettes have
> never been ordered against it.

**Candlesticks used as ARCHITECTURE, deliberately empty** `[04:33]`: *"You'll also notice I did
not put candles in here, did I? No, I did not because I'm using these as an architectural shape
as opposed to a candle situation."*

**Six finishes on one 914 mm surface, named one at a time** `[04:48]`-`[05:31]`: rattan (woven) ·
white books (smooth matte) · brushed brass (luster) · sea coral (organic) · green plant
(organic) · soft grey pot. Our vignettes carry two or three.

**A plant is chosen for its LEAF MASS, not its species** `[03:58]`: *"the scale of the coral is
very airy, while the scale of the leaves of this particular plant, I didn't pick one that was
really lacy and open. I chose one that had the solid."*

**FLOW = repeating a material across the room** `[05:31]`-`[06:01]`: *"the gold here connects
with the gold here, which connects with… the gold rim on this table… So, another principle is
flow. Repeating a matter."*

**A finish family is a hard constraint, and she REMOVES an object over it** `[13:51]`: *"see how
the shiny finish of this — these are like antique brass and this is super shiny brass. So we are
going to put that guy away."*

**A large-footprint vessel can replace the tray** `[18:28]`: *"I don't have a tray under this,
but I didn't need it. This almost creates a tray effect."*

**The vessel is FILLED, and the filling is dressed** (frame 0212, `[15:52]`-`[16:26]`): sand
poured around the candle base inside the hurricane, brushed off the top with a makeup brush,
then *"a little grouping"* of shells — explicitly **not all the way around**. Our glass vessels
are empty.

**Scale is chained to the FURNITURE, not to the table** `[19:15]`: *"the scale of what's on here
matches the scale of the size of the table AND the larger furniture pieces… the pieces of
furniture around it are large."* And the pairing rule `[18:12]`: *"when you have a very large
vase you're going to use, you want a very large candle."*

**A named defect for a room that reads bought rather than lived in** `[09:02]`: *"people go and
they're just looking for FILLERS to fill a space and it lacks the meaningfulness."*

## 3. STYLING FOR A LENS — `88ipoYZ_TdY`

Paige Wassel, *"my job as a prop stylist (come on set with me to style a photoshoot)"*, 16.9 min,
transcript + 44 frames. A freelance prop stylist on a furniture-brand shoot. **This is the video
the whole curriculum was missing**: the job is not to make a room, it is to make a photograph.

**The object is oriented TO THE CAMERA** `[12:14]`: *"throw it and like tilt it towards us kind
of."* And the frame is the unit of speech: *"am I in frame to say goodbye"*, *"move one like here
right here over to the front."*

**The styling only resolves in the picture** `[11:10]`: *"I wish I could show you the photos
because that's where you really see it come together."*

**The whole candidate pool is laid out and looked at before anything is placed** (frames 0093,
0128): twenty-plus plants and vessels on trestle tables, chosen from in one pass. She also marks
plants in the greenhouse with blue tape `[05:07]` so the crew packs the shortlist.

**A matched set is deliberately refused** `[08:13]`, `[15:12]`, `[15:51]`: *"it's gonna be a
mixture of glasses so it's not consistent, it's all about mixing products" · "adding in some
different types of glassware because everything's different" · "the mismatch is so cute."*

**The STATE is mid-event, not tidy** `[10:34]`: *"we're gonna set this up as if people are here
for a party. I want it to look kind of messy."* Frame 0154 carries the evidence: two coupes
already poured. **Our rooms contain no evidence of a person.**

**An object is removed because its colour is at budget** `[15:12]`: *"I'm not serving wine at
this function mainly because there's too much yellow and our wine is yellow."*

**Height on a table comes from a plant with a candlestick pushed into its soil** `[14:11]`:
*"what's fun for outdoor plants is sticking the candle straight into the plant."* Frame 0189
shows the same pot's soil packed with moss — **every vessel's top surface is dressed.**

**Pillows are the cheapest lever and are swapped continuously** `[10:00]`: *"I'm constantly on
set switching out pillows because they make such a huge difference."*

**Order on set** `[08:34]`: furniture is pulled into position first, then styled — and the
furniture position itself is revised against the frame (*"okay I'm changing my mind, let's put
the couch in front of the door"*).

## 4. A BEDROOM MADE TO READ FINISHED WITH SEVEN PURCHASES — `9MPpF6UmQmc`

Caroline Winkler, 30.7 min. A real bedroom that its owner calls *"bland… my least favourite
room"* — our verdict in a stranger's words — fixed with a counted, bounded buy.

**STEP ONE IS SUBTRACTION** `[03:29]`-`[03:47]`: *"Step one, remove all the wall decor from your
room. All the knick-knacks, all the clutter, the embellishments, the adornments… It is really
hard to reimagine your space when there is stuff all over the walls. Get it out."*
> Every styling round this studio has run has been additive. This is the same shape as the bed
> grammar's *"take the wrinkles out first, then put the softness back"*, at room scale.

**THE BED IS HALF THE ROOM, AND ITS CLOTH IS WHY THE ROOM READS AS NOTHING** `[15:13]`-`[15:37]`:
*"It is the single largest, most front and center item in your bedroom, and every single one of
y'all are leaving it white or gray or freaking nothing… And then you have the gall to say, 'Why
does it feel like nothing in this room?' Well, what makes up 50% of it?"*
> A fourth independent voice for the bed carrying the room, arriving while our own bed cloth is
> a signed procurement gap and our duvet is neutral.

**"Black is not neutral"** `[11:39]`: *"I know you boys think that black is neutral, that it's a
nothing, that it's not making a choice. It's a choice… black is very intense. It's very
commanding, especially when it's just a giant black square."*

**Decor density is spread, not bought** `[17:51]`: *"there was a lot of busyness built into just
the window side of the room and then the rest was kind of empty… we can kill two birds with one
stone by spreading out some of that wall decor and sharing the love."* The fix for an empty wall
is to MOVE existing decor.

**A rug goes over wall-to-wall carpet** `[04:56]`: its job is colour, pattern and texture, not
floor covering — *"hands down, rugs are the biggest item I see bachelors overlook."*

**Paint only the trim and the doors** `[13:45]`: one quart, one day, *"frame out the room… still
maintain all the lightness of the light coloured walls."*

## 5. THAI PRACTICE, FROM A DESIGNER IN A CLIENT'S HOUSE — `m3UoGzkcYD4`

dooDeco, *เซียนแต่งให้ EP.2*, 12 min, at a Centro Ramintra 2 detached house. It is a built-in
walkthrough rather than a decor-placement lesson, and that is recorded plainly — but it carries
four Thai-practice facts nothing on disk has.

**บานเพ่ — the routed finger-groove door front, named** `[03:52]`: *"บานเพ่ก็คือตู้ที่มีลักษณะการออกแบบ
หน้าบานให้มีร่องเว้า โดยที่มีการเซาะร่องหรือทำเป็นส่วนเว้าเข้าไปในตัวหน้าบานตู้ เพื่อให้เราสามารถใช้นิ้วจับแบบเปิดหรือว่าปิด
โดยที่ไม่ต้องมีมือจับยื่นออกมา"* — the Thai trade term for a handle-less front with a routed recess,
recommended for *"บ้านแนวเรียบหรูหรือมินิมอล"*. Our wardrobe fronts have no pulls and no groove, and
this names the detail that resolves it.

**The built-in cell is dimensioned from the client's OWN appliances** `[04:46]`-`[05:19]`: they
ask the size of the robot vacuum, the cat feeder, the fridge and the TV before drawing the cell —
*"เราก็จะวัดระยะจากของพวกนั้นน่ะแหละ… เราก็จะออกแบบมาไม่หลวมไม่แน่นมากจนเกินไป"*. R10 stated from the Thai
trade side: a cell exists because a named object goes in it.

**A horizontal line is DERIVED from the neighbouring top, not typed** `[07:28]`-`[07:41]`: the
adjacent table top sits at ~750 mm and the headboard's line was set to meet it — *"ให้มันร้อยกันมา
แล้วมันเจอกันพอดี มันจะได้ดูไม่ขัดตาแบบผิดระยะกัน"*. R9's law spoken by a practitioner.

**An open cell justified by clothes not yet washed** `[08:56]`-`[09:09]`: *"ตู้โล่งตัวนี้อาจจะไว้
เสื้อผ้าที่ยังไม่ได้ซัก… เหมาะสำหรับคนที่มีเสื้อโค้ท เสื้อสูท ที่ใส่ซ้ำแล้วมันไม่อับ"*. A **second** independent Thai
source for the open-cell use, promoting §8.7's single-source line to two.

**And a market fact about process** `[10:45]`: the client brought a clear reference, the first
image came back *"90%"* right, and the whole job closed in *"2-3 ครั้ง"* of adjustment.
