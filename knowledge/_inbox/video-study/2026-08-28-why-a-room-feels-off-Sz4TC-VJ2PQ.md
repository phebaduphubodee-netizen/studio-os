**Why it was picked.** Two judge lanes chose it independently. It is a G3 rung: the

## 1. THE THESIS SENTENCE, and it is our defect in six words

`[27:54]` *"I want to make it feel intentional. I want to make it feel like it's not an
accident."*

That is the whole distinction between our frame and the delivered pool. Our room is not
missing objects so much as it is missing the read that somebody **chose** them. The panel
scores that gap as 25-40 "half-made" against 80-100; this is the sentence that names what
the score is measuring.

Related, `[19:31]`, a submitter's own words describing a room a designer then calls 90%
finished: *"this space never feels done"* — the same complaint from the other side.

## 2. RULES STATED AS RULES — the ones that carry a number or a threshold

### 2.1 THE LAMP RULE, and it lands on a defect we have already measured

`[18:05]` — Molly: *"you should consider either wall sconces or two very substantial
fixtures to go on the nightstands"*; Caroline: *"I like that you said substantial, because
**everybody goes too small on their bedside table lamps. If whatever size you pick, it's
too small.** Go like two sizes bigger"*; Molly: *"I promise you."*

**Two designers agreeing, unprompted, on one object we have in frame.** Our own numbers
say the same thing from the other direction: C3 measured our centre figure assembly at
**0.65× the target's area**, and the frame carries **109 masses under 24 px**. This is
independent, outside confirmation that under-scaled accessories are a *recurring
professional failure mode*, not a quirk of our build — and the correction they give is not
"measure it again", it is **go two sizes up**.

Same rule for the accessory on a table, `[14:11]`-`[14:41]`: *"the bowl on the coffee
table… I would suggest getting something more substantial — teeny tiny, it is little"* and
then the reason: *"having the correct bowl or the correct vase almost feels like a
frivolous thing, because it's not the sofa and it's not the rug… but it ridiculously
really can tie the space together as the finishing piece. It's kind of like having the tux
without the bow tie. It IS actually a focal point."*

### 2.2 THE RUG — a size, a shape ban, and a role

`[05:14]` *"that rug is a little bit small — let's expand the rug. **Maybe don't go round.
It's hard to make a round rug work.** … I would get a larger, probably **9×12** rug at
least."*
`[18:34]` on a grey-and-white rug in a cold room: *"I would resell the carpet, because it
is just adding more grey and more white and it's not adding any warmth"* — and the rug is
called out as *"the largest thing"* in the room.
`[27:27]` *"an area rug helps to define the zone"*, and it is legitimate to **layer an
area rug on top of existing carpet**.

Bearing on ORD-2026-08-25b (*"พรมยังไม่สวย"*): the rug is judged first by SIZE, then by
whether it carries WARMTH, and only then by pattern. Ours is being judged on edge geometry.

### 2.3 A PROPORTION CONSTANT for anything mounted above something

`[23:11]` *"the fireplace is the exact same width as the TV — to me that's the killer…
speaking in the constants of design proportions, ideally a TV up there would be a little
bit **smaller**. It ends up feeling kind of overwhelming; it won't feel balanced."*

A mounted object must be **narrower than the element it sits on**. Directly testable
against our art/mirror over casework from the scene dump, no render needed.

## 3. FIVE NAMED DEFECT CLASSES — the vocabulary this builder does not have

| Named defect | Timestamp | The words |
|---|---|---|
| **"a train of furniture"** | `[17:36]` | *"anytime it's a train of furniture, let's try and just put some space between things"* |
| **every wall lined with furniture** | `[24:33]` | *"you don't want every wall in a space to be completely covered in furniture — you want there to be some **breathing room**"*; and `[24:07]` *"versus everyone just sitting up against the wall"* |
| **"plastered against the wall"** | `[22:41]` | *"it's plastered against the wall — we've got to give it some wiggle room"* |
| **all-flat / all-same-kind** | `[15:40]` | *"when it's just art print, art print, art print, art print — just all flat things on the wall — that falls flat. When it's just all shelves everywhere, that can fall flat."* The fix named is *"something **sculptural and three-dimensional**"* `[16:08]` |
| **"the room feels cold"** | `[18:05]` `[26:01]` | cause given as *"there's no color in this room, that's why it feels cold — there's not a single color to be found"*, and the counter-rule `[17:36]`: *"anything wood, any natural material is immediately going to bring warmth"* |

> **The one that should sting.** Our wardrobe wall is nine cells of the same kind of thing
> in a grid, in front of a wood wall of the same kind of thing, and the room is judged
> cold and flat. *"All flat things on the wall — that falls flat"* and *"when it's just
> all shelves everywhere, that can fall flat"* is a designer describing that failure
> without ever having seen it. The prescribed fix is not more of the same at better
> quality; it is **a different KIND of object** — sculptural, three-dimensional, off the
> wall (`[16:37]`: *"a tree… it has texture, it's three-dimensional, it's off the wall"*).

Also, twice: **zoning before objects** — `[03:20]` *"this is a long room, the number one
thing you want to do is split it up into zones"*, and `[25:02]` *"I would almost pause on
getting any side tables until you try this new layout, because the size of the side tables
really are going to be dependent on how this new layout feels."* Layout decides object
size; object size is not chosen first.

## 4. THE FRAME THAT IS WORTH MORE THAN THE TRANSCRIPT

`frame_0169` (≈ `[06:11]`, the wall the submitter calls *"awful"*) shows one ordinary
bedroom wall carrying, in a single view: **a wall-mounted split AC head, a thermostat, two
switch plates, a wall speaker, and a door with visible hardware.**

Our render contains **none of these** — no aircon, no door, no socket, no switch plate,
all four filed by critics on the P2r-29 SENSE list. This frame is the positive control: a
real bedroom wall is *dense with services*, and the designers' answer is never to remove
them. It is to integrate them — `[09:54]` *"for light switches and outlets you can replace
the plates, get a switch plate cover"*, and a deeper frame to sit over the speaker
`[10:47]`. That is the **third independent designer in this curriculum treating a switch
plate as an object with a face** (see also `kmCHv3PG2XM` on cords).
