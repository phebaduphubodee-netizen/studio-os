## 1. THE WIRING IS CUT INTO THE CARCASS BEFORE ANYTHING IS ASSEMBLED

`AbmVfYx8aTo` `[00:54]`:
> *"because I did want to **illuminate these shelves with the minimum amount of exposed
> wires**, I did have to give a lot of consideration to that **during the initial
> construction**. Right now I'm **cutting recesses and grooves for all the wiring and
> connectors** that will eventually connect up all the LED strips that go into this."*

> **Third independent source on lighting-in-joinery, and the one that adds the half the
> others left out.** `AAoo_VwqQZc` gave the dado for the extrusion; John Cullen gave the
> reason (conceal the source). This gives the *wiring path*: recesses and connector pockets
> routed into the carcass **before assembly**, because afterwards there is nowhere for the
> cable to go.
>
> Our casework LED strips have no cable route anywhere in the model — which is the same
> defect class as *"lamps with no cords"* on the P2r-29(c) SENSE list, one level deeper
> inside the millwork. An illuminated shelf with no groove behind it is not a shelf that
> could be wired.

## 2. THE CARCASS, IN NUMBERS

| Item | Stated | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| sheet goods | `[00:54]` *"**eleven sheets of three quarter inch plywood**"* | 19 mm ply |
| solid stock | `[00:04]` *"about **130 board feet of white oak**"* | — |
| **module depths, MIXED** | `[02:29]` *"seven separate modules… **four of these modules ended up being a full 20 inches deep while three stayed at 13 inches deep**"* | **508 mm** and **330 mm** |
| **face frame width** | `[04:05]` *"I ripped the white oak into **one and a half inch wide strips**"* | **38 mm** |
| drawer boxes | `[05:56]` *"**half inch thick birch plywood**… for the larger drawers I probably should have used three quarter"* | 12.7 mm (19 mm better) |
| drawer bottoms | `[06:46]` *"the bottoms are **quarter inch** plywood"*, glued in | 6.4 mm |
| drawer dividers | `[06:46]` *"the **34 inch wide** drawers having a **divider** in them seemed like a good idea"* | 864 mm → divided |

Three construction facts worth carrying:

- **Edge banding** `[01:41]`: *"any **exposed plywood edges** were treated with **edge
  banding** so it would look like solid wood."* A ply carcass shows its core on every cut
  edge unless banded — a visible material boundary our model does not have.
- **Pre-finished before assembly** `[02:29]`: *"all the cabinet pieces were **pre-sanded and
  pre-stained before they were assembled**"*, using a white gel stain over oak. This is why
  a real built-in has consistent colour into its corners.
- **Face frames are a separate member** `[04:05]`, joined with pocket screws and glued where
  screws would show. Our cells are boxes; a real one has a 38 mm frame across the front.

> **The mixed depths are the item to take seriously.** Seven modules, two depths, chosen
> *"due to the space considerations of the room"*. Our nine cells are a uniform grid — and
> uniformity is exactly what four separate critics in this curriculum have named as the tell
> (*"all flat things on the wall"*, *"you wouldn't have just one type of vegetation"*,
> *"they're all placed at the same scale, rotation"*). A real built-in wall is **modules of
> different depths and widths solving a real room**, and it reads that way.

## 3. THE CEILING CURTAIN POCKET — and it knows the wardrobe is there

`XeCPWJgb4Qg` is the same detail as `saFsrHObKyk` and `22ijpgjmkWY`, but framed on site with
a carpenter narrating, and it supplies the two things the others did not.

### 3.1 THE POCKET IS FRAMED BEFORE THE CEILING IS LINED
`[00:01]` *"the idea is that **the curtain tracks sit up into the ceiling** rather than being
attached to the wall as it would be on a normal house. It's a little bit of work, but it's
actually **fairly easy to do it now rather than doing it after the place is lined**. We
almost forgot — we started lining the ceiling and then got to do these."*

### 3.2 THE SETBACK, WITH A NUMBER
`[00:44]` *"I've got my **300** mark here. I just want to go back a little bit from that. At
least **318** I'm doing. **325** or something just to give us a bit of room to play with."*

**A pocket set back on the order of 300-325 mm from the wall face.** The first real
dimension for the recess our curtains do not have.

### 3.3 THE POCKET BOX MUST BE CARRIED AT ITS END — the hang-rail law again
`[01:27]` *"these boxes are going to **hang from the trusses**, but the **trusses are at
900s**. So in this case here, we would have our box hanging out about **800 past its last
fixing point**. So what I'm going to do is **put a block so that we've got fixing right above
the end of it.**"*

Trusses at **900 mm** centres; a blocking piece added so the box is fixed **at its end**
rather than cantilevering 800 mm. That is the identical rule already recorded for the hang
rail (`iJYto0muSd4`) and for shelves (`axVhLMkgq2U`): **a member is carried at its ends, or
it is given an intermediate support.** Three trades, one law, and our rails, our curtains
and our shelves all break it.

He also gives the plasterboard failure mode `[00:44]`: *"I want to make sure that we're a
couple of **Rondo outlets fixed right at the edges**, otherwise your **gib's going to sag**
and then your **joints are going to end up cracking**."* — the ceiling sheet needs support
at the pocket's edges.

### 3.4 THE LINE THAT SHOULD CHANGE OUR MODEL
`[02:11]`, on a walk-in wardrobe:
> *"This is a walk-in wardrobe and we're doing things a little bit differently. We're going
> **full width for this one rather than just having 3/4 width, which would look a little bit
> silly**. And **the cabinets in here will come out 100 out from the wall so that the
> curtains can tuck in behind it.** So it'll end up looking really tidy."*

Two decisions in one sentence, and our frame contains both objects with **no relationship
between them**:

1. **The pocket runs the FULL WIDTH of the wall**, not the width of the window — because a
   partial run *"would look a little bit silly."*
2. **The wardrobe casework stands 100 mm off the wall so the curtain passes behind it.**

Our curtains vanish into the ceiling slab beside a full-height wardrobe wall, and nothing in
the spec says how the two meet. Here is a carpenter solving exactly that junction with a
100 mm offset and a full-width pocket. This is the single most directly applicable detail
found so far for the curtain half of P2r-29.

## 4. WHAT IS OWED FROM THIS UNIT

1. **Route the LED wiring** as recesses and connector pockets in the carcass (§1) — an
   illuminated shelf with no cable path is the millwork version of a lamp with no cord.
2. **Give the wardrobe modules more than one depth** (§2) — 508/330 mm in the reference;
   ours is a uniform grid, which is the tell four critics have already named.
3. **Add the members a real carcass has**: a ~38 mm face frame, edge banding on every
   exposed ply edge, drawer boxes with a stated thickness and dividers over ~860 mm.
4. **Curtain pocket: set back ~300-325 mm, run FULL WALL WIDTH, and hold the wardrobe
   100 mm off the wall so the curtain tucks behind it** (§3.4).
5. **Carry the pocket box at its ends** with blocking where the structure does not land
   there (§3.3) — the same rule as the rail and the shelf, now from a third trade.
