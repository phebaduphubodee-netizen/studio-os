## 1. THE PRESENTATION IS A SIGN-OFF, AND IT IS PHYSICAL

`[00:48]` *"everything is **chosen, specified, priced up** — we're just doing **one last
presentation** to the client before they hopefully **sign everything off**."*

The artefact is a set of **trays, one per room**, holding every hard finish and every fabric:
`[00:00]` *"we lay out all the fabrics and all the finishes into the trays… this means that
they all look really neat and tidy and **the clients can actually touch and feel every single
material and surface** that they'll have in their property, to make sure that they're happy
with those."*

Craft details of the tray itself, which is a deliverable and is treated as one:
- fabrics *"all get **folded over neatly**"*, laid **in order** — *"you might have like
  upholstery fabrics and then, if there's lots of cushions, **a row of cushion fabrics**"*;
- *"just making sure there's **no little bits of fluff**"*;
- between presentations everything lives in labelled plastic folders, *"everything's labelled
  by the **project and the room type**"*.

> **For our next unit this is the shape of the answer to "what does a client actually
> receive".** It is not a render. It is a per-room, touchable, priced, signed-off material
> set — and the render exists to show what that set becomes.

## 2. THREE PIECES OF DESIGN REASONING HEARD OUT LOUD

### 2.1 A ROOM OF GLASS AND WOOD READS COLD, AND THE FIX IS TEXTILE
`[03:12]`, on a TV room: *"just to warm it up a little bit, because it's got **quite a cold
feel** when you're in that space — **there's no soft furnishings, it's got a lot of glass, a
lot of wood** — we're going to add this **linen wallpaper**"*, plus *"a lot of **bouclé**,
really heavily textured fabrics."*

> **That is our room described by someone who has never seen it**: a lot of wood, judged
> cold. Their remedy is not more wood and not a colour change — it is **textile mass**. It
> agrees with Caroline Winkler's *"everything is very hard… very rigid"* and with the
> material-spectrum source's rule that a soft-end palette needs something from elsewhere on
> the axis. **Three sources, one prescription.**

### 2.2 A MATERIAL IS CHOSEN AGAINST WHAT IT SITS IN FRONT OF
`[04:53]`, on bar-stool leather: *"when you're picking the leather fabrics you have to be
quite careful… **you always have to think what it's going to be in front of**. It's quite an
**insipid** sort of taupe — sometimes it looks pink, sometimes it looks grey — so **if you
don't get a really clear contrast**, the bar stool could **clash** with that. So by going
white, you know it's going to have a **definite contrast** there."*

A colour is selected for its **contrast against its own backdrop**, not on its own merits.
Every object in our frame sits in front of a wood wall and none has been chosen that way.

### 2.3 COUNTS ARE LOW, AND A SCHEME CAN LOOK POOR ON THE BOARD
`[04:53]` *"just keeping it **very minimal on the cushions — we've only got three cushions in
this entire room**."*
`[03:12]` on a neutral guest bedroom: *"it's more about the **form**… very serene and simple.
**In a tray, like you say, that doesn't look that impressive.**"*

Two useful correctives: a delivered room can be **sparse** on decorative objects, and a
scheme that photographs poorly as a flat-lay can still be the right room. Weigh this against
the archviz reviewer's *"my closet's stuffed with stuff"* — **the sparse/full answer differs
by object class**, and neither is a general rule.

## 3. THE CD SET — the sheet order, from a certified designer

`pBy70zgtPIU` `[00:48]`: construction documents are *"a set of drawings used to tell the
contractor and other tradespeople **how to build a space** according to the intended
design"*, they demonstrate code compliance, and `[01:37]` they are what the client **sends
out to bid** to price the work.

The order of a set:

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| **cover / title sheet** (A0.0) | project name and location, **drawing index**, location map, drawing notes, site info — *"some designers often forget about or neglect to include a cover sheet"* |
| A0.x | fire exiting and egress plans, safety notes, code information, existing plans |
| **demolition** (D or A1) | demo floor plans, demo RCPs, and a **demolition key plan** explaining line styles and symbols |
| **construction** (A2 / C / P) | new floor plans **then** RCPs — *"we always place all the floor plans first before the RCP sheets"* — plus a construction key plan |
| **finish plans** (A3) | flooring, wall base, paint colours, ceiling types; symbols correlate to the finish schedule |
| **elevations → sections → details** (A4, A5, in that order) | mounting heights on shelves, partition sections, **millwork details** |
| **schedules** | finish schedule *"down to even what the **paint sheen** should be — is it going to be glossy, is it going to be eggshell"*; **lighting schedule** with fixture specifications; plumbing schedule; accessory schedule; door and window schedules |
| other trades | **S** structural · **M** mechanical · **P** plumbing · **E** electrical, drafted by their engineers and incorporated by the architect |

The numbering rule that survives every naming system `[03:14]`: *"whatever numbering system
you're using, **pages must always be numbered in order**"*, and the point of the names is
only *"so that the set is easy to read and pages are easy to find."*

### 3.1 THE ITEM THAT LANDS ON THE CURRENT FRAME
**The RCP — reflected ceiling plan — is a required sheet of every set**, sitting immediately
after the floor plans, and the **lighting schedule** is a required schedule listing every
fixture with its specifications.

Our room's twelve downlights are an RCP artefact with **no RCP and no lighting schedule
behind them**. Four separate sources in this curriculum say the ceiling grid is the defect;
this one says what should have produced the layout instead, and that the fixture list is a
signed document rather than a scattering.

`[07:14]` also names the division of labour to settle at the start: *"at the beginning of
the project have an **open conversation with the architect** and create a **clear division of
roles and responsibilities**"* — and the scope limit that decides it for an interior
designer: interior non-structural partitions, finishes, furniture and lighting.

## 4. WHAT IS OWED FROM THIS UNIT

1. For the real-client run: the client receives **per-room material trays, priced and signed
   off**, and a **CD set in the order in §3**. Neither exists as a stage output in our
   contracts today.
2. **Add an RCP and a lighting schedule** to the current project before the next lighting
   round — the grid has no document behind it.
3. **Choose object colours against their backdrop** (§2.2); ours sit against wood and none
   was selected that way.
4. **Test the cold-room prescription** (§2.1): the fix for a wood-and-glass room reading cold
   is textile mass, not more wood and not a hue change.
5. Note the sparse-versus-full tension (§2.3) rather than adopting either as a rule.
