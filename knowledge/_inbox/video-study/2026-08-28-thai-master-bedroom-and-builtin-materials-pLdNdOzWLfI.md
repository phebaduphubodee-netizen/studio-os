## 4. WHAT A THAI BUILT-IN IS ACTUALLY MADE OF — and a tension we must decide

`LsjpbtEeKfc` splits built-in furniture into **carcass material** and **finishing**, then
into three carcass families:

1. **ไม้จริง — solid wood.** Teak (ไม้สัก), ไม้แดง/ประดู่ for luxurious or Chinese rooms, **oak**
   for modern work; at accessible prices, **ash** or **rubberwood (ยางพารา)**. Finished by
   staining — clear to show grain on teak, or stained darker/grey/light on rubberwood.
   *Pro:* durability, though for plantation timber, rot and termite resistance depends on
   whether it was pressure-treated (อัดน้ำยา). *Con:* expensive, and `[03:49]` **"ช่างฝีมือใน
   การทำงานไม้จริง ๆ… มันหาค่อนข้างยาก ยิ่งงานสีเอง"** — skilled joiners, and especially skilled
   finishers, are hard to find, so the finish may not come out crisp.
2. **เพาะโครง — a built-up frame** faced front and back with plywood or thin board, then
   either painted to look like solid wood, or faced with **HPL (High Pressure Laminate)** —
   *"ถ้าเป็นแบรนด์ที่เราคุ้นเคย ก็อาจจะเป็นพวกโฟเมก้า… แต่จริง ๆ แล้วโฟเมก้าเป็นชื่อแบรนด์ ตัววัสดุเราจะเรียกว่า
   **HPL**"*. Cheaper than solid; still needs a skilled installer to cut and finish edges.
3. **เฟอร์นิเจอร์บอร์ด — engineered board.** Particle board (lowest; the chips are visible on the
   edge), **MDF** (finely ground, dense, brown), and **MDF grade HMR** — *High Moisture
   Resistance*, and the identification tip is that **HMR board is dyed GREEN** `[07:30]`.
   Finishing: painted directly (MDF's smooth face is why **high-gloss furniture is usually
   MDF/HMR**), HPL, or **factory melamine-faced board**, which is now the popular route.

### 4.1 THE ANSWER TO "WHICH MATERIAL GETS THE JOB ABANDONED"
`[11:00]`-`[13:00]`, and this is the video's whole point: solid-wood and painted/HPL work
require many skilled sub-trades, so the contractor carries risk and jobs get walked away
from. **Factory melamine-faced board is cut, drilled, edge-banded and finished in the
factory and only ASSEMBLED on site** — *"เวลามาถึงหน้างานครับ เค้าก็จะแค่ทำการ**ประกอบ**เฉย ๆ"* — so
*"**ความเสี่ยงต่อการถูกทิ้งงานจะน้อย**"*.

How to check a supplier `[12:30]`: **ask to visit the factory**; a real one has *"เครื่องหลัก ๆ
อยู่สัก **4-5 ตัว** มี**เครื่องตัด** มี**เครื่องเจาะ** มี**เครื่องปิดขอบ**"* — a panel saw, a boring machine, an
**edge-bander** — buys pre-faced melamine board, and has references. The rule he closes on:
*"**อะไรก็ตามที่หลีกเลี่ยงแรงงานเยอะ ๆ ได้ ความเสี่ยงของการที่จะถูกทิ้งงานมันก็จะลดลง**"*.

> **THE TENSION WE MUST DECIDE, not resolve here.** The palette source in this same
> curriculum (`5gfcLreD2ZM`) states a hard rule — no surface printed to look like another
> material. The Thai market's default built-in surface is **melamine or HPL with a printed
> wood grain**. Both are true, and our wardrobe is a Thai built-in.
>
> That has a direct consequence for P2r-2. If our wall is melamine-faced board, then its
> repeat is not a veneer flitch drifting leaf to leaf — it is a **printed décor paper with a
> fixed repeat length**, and the correct fix is the *décor's* repeat and rotation, not
> book-versus-slip matching. **We do not currently know which material our wall is supposed
> to be**, and the two answers imply different generators. That is a question for the owner
> (an engineer, who judges built quality) or his designer friend, and it belongs beside
> VG-09.

## 5. THE THAI BEDROOM RULES, and one thin pick

`SMI9meHxrIo` (ลุงช่าง), beyond the headboard rule already quoted:

- **Orientation** `[00:00]` *"เราจะวางไว้ทาง**ทิศตะวันออกหรือทิศใต้** เหตุผลเพราะเรา**หลีกเลี่ยงที่จะให้ห้องนอน
  โดนแสงแดดตอนบ่าย** ซึ่งมันแรง และมัน**จะสะสมความร้อนไว้ถึงตอนกลางคืน**"* — bedrooms east or south;
  avoid west, because the afternoon sun is fierce and the heat carries into the night.
  **A climate reason for a sun direction, which our daylight portal has never had.**
- **Placement** `[00:43]`: away from noise; near a main road, put the bedroom at the back.
- **A master bedroom is three functions** `[02:09]`: sleeping + bathroom + **dressing area**,
  with the bathroom kept as far from the bed as possible for noise and smell. (Our room is
  the sleeping + dressing pair.)
- **Only necessary furniture**, and a bed sized to the room — *"ไม่ใหญ่เกินไป ไม่เล็กเกินไป"*;
  ideally **no TV** — *"ไม่มีทีวีเนี่ยจะเป็นห้องนอนที่สมบูรณ์แบบ"*.
- **Flooring** `[03:40]`: avoid tile in a bedroom — cold and slippery, worse with elderly
  occupants; prefer real wood or parquet, else laminate, engineered wood, or vinyl,
  *"ให้ความรู้สึกที่มัน**ซอฟต์เท้า**หน่อย"* — softer underfoot. **Our wood floor is the right family
  for a Thai bedroom**, which nothing in the spec has ever said.

**`X7ZpE_YGgGk` is thin and is recorded as such**: it is a Thai woodworker arguing that
people should take a workshop course rather than learn from YouTube — motivational and
promotional, with no dimensions, no detail and no critique. It contributes one line worth
keeping about this market, `[00:00]`: *"ปัญหาหน้างานมัน**ไม่เหมือนที่เราคุยกันไว้**"* — on-site
reality does not match what was agreed — which is the same risk the materials video prices.
No re-pick.

## 6. WHAT IS OWED FROM THIS UNIT

1. **Decide what our wardrobe is MADE OF** (§4.1) — Thai melamine/HPL board or veneered
   solid — because P2r-2's correct fix differs between them. Ask the owner; it is a
   constructability question and he is an engineer.
2. **Give the open cell a USE** (§2.1): tomorrow's clothes, a bag display, a laundry basket
   niche, a fridge. R10 asks every mass to justify itself and this is the first source that
   justifies an open cell at all.
3. **Concealed LED uplight above the headboard** (§2.3) — now three independent sightings,
   and it answers "bright with no source" for the one wall the camera looks at.
4. **Check the headboard wall reads solid** (§2.4) — a stated Thai rule, satisfied in the
   reference by making a window concealable rather than by removing it.
5. **Justify the sun direction** (§5): east or south for a Thai bedroom, never west.
6. Candidate details, all cheap: a **300 mm bed plinth**, **long integrated door pulls**,
   **V-grooves** to break a plain wall, **wall niches** for objects, and a **two-colour
   door** where two rooms meet.
