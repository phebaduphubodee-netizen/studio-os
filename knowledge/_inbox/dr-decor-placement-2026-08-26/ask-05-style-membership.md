### Part 2: Practical Sourced Asset Filtering Workflows

Professional interior stylists and staging directors utilize several documented
screening methodologies to filter a sprawling digital asset library down to one
coherent style.

#### Workflow 1: The 80/20 Rule for Style Blending
To prevent a styled environment from looking sterile, cold, or like a "themed 
showroom", stylists use the **80/20 Rule** to manage the balance between the 
primary aesthetic and secondary accents [62].
*   **`"Island Style Home Decor Tips... | Lili Alessandra"`**: 
    > *"The idea is that 80% of your decor should adhere to your main theme—in 
this case, island style with its natural materials and casual furniture. The 
final 20% can be something different that adds a layer of personality. This 
could be a sleek, modern floor lamp, a few antique pieces, or some 
industrial-style metal accents."* [62]

#### Workflow 2: The 70/20/10 Color Proportioning Matrix
When selecting accessory colors from a library, professionals do not pair tones
randomly. They follow a strict ratio where a dominant neutral background allows
the high-impact decorative pieces to read as luxury [11, 63]:
*   **`"Contemporary Modern Interior Design Singapore | 6AM Spaces"`**: 
    > *"The 70/20/10 approach works well: 70% neutral, 20% secondary tones, and
10% bold accents for striking contrast and character."* [11]

#### Workflow 3: The Mood-Board Motif Analysis (Sourcing Hero Items)
For story-driven residential renders, stylists filter through digital libraries
by first mapping recurring motifs from a broad collage before testing key 
candidate pieces in mock layouts [64, 65].
*   **`"One-of-a-Kind Interior Statements for Story-Driven Homes – Kevin 
Francis Design"`**: 
    > *"The fastest reliable method: collect 30–50 images you love, find the 
recurring motifs, name one anchor object archetype, then test it in a mock 
layout... look for patterns in color temperature (warm or cool?), era 
(pre-industrial, mid-century, contemporary?), texture (rough and tactile or 
smooth and reflective?), and scale (intimate or grand?). Three or four motifs 
will repeat. Those are your design DNA."* [64, 65]

#### Workflow 4: The Prop-Vetting Questionnaire (Sourcing Culling Filters)
When culling through an extensive model database, stylists apply a strict, 
three-part quality control filter to discard assets that look flat on camera 
[66, 67]:
*   **`"Props Designer - Musical Theater Guild - MIT Wiki Service"`**: 
    > *"Any item that does not meet the criteria below should not be kept: 1. 
Is this item in good condition? If not, is it easily fixable? 2. Is this item 
composed of any perishable materials, or materials that will 
discolor/degrade/otherwise become of poor condition over time? 3. Is this item 
useful?"* [66]
