Here is the breakdown of the standard residential interior design phases, the 
anatomy of a construction document set, and the critical client sign-off 
milestones, based on the provided architectural and interior design sources.

### **1. Phases of a Residential Interior Design Engagement**
The standard framework for executing a design project follows five primary 
phases, typically preceded by an essential Pre-Design/Programming stage [1-3].

*   **Pre-Design / Programming (Feasibility & Problem-Seeking):** Before actual
design begins, the architect or designer researches the project, evaluates 
existing conditions, and defines the scope of work [3, 4]. 
    *   *Deliverables:* Site analysis, existing condition "as-built" models, 
bubble diagrams outlining spatial relationships, a summarized list of required 
spaces (building program), and a preliminary project budget [5-8].
*   **Schematic Design (SD) (~15% of effort):** The exploratory phase where the
fundamental direction, form, scale, and spatial organization of the project are
established [9-11].
    *   *Deliverables:* Preliminary floor plans, basic 3D concepts/sketches, 
and a rough order of magnitude (ROM) preliminary cost estimate to ensure the 
project aligns with the budget [11-13].
*   **Design Development (DD) (~20% of effort):** The approved schematic 
concept is refined with greater specificity. The layout is solidified, and 
actual materials and systems are chosen [9, 14, 15]. 
    *   *Deliverables:* Expanded and detailed floor plans, interior elevations,
daylighting/solar studies (if applicable), and final selections of materials, 
finishes, fixtures, and appliances [15-17].
*   **Construction Documents (CD) (~40% of effort):** The most extensive phase,
where the design transitions into highly detailed, technical documentation used
for permitting and building [9, 18-20].
    *   *Deliverables:* A complete, coordinated set of architectural drawings 
and a finalized specification package. Typically, two sets are produced: a 
"Permit set" for the local jurisdiction and an "Issue for Construction" set for
the contractors [21-23].
*   **Bidding (~5% of effort):** The designer assists the client in selecting a
general contractor by evaluating competitive or negotiated bids [9, 24-26].
    *   *Deliverables:* Clarifications to the construction documents, responses
to contractor questions, and the final contractor proposal/construction 
agreement [24-26].
*   **Construction Administration (CA) (~20% of effort):** The designer 
oversees the construction process as a representative of the design intent, 
ensuring the builder follows the specifications [9, 27, 28].
    *   *Deliverables:* Approved contractor submittals (shop drawings, product 
data, samples), responses to Requests for Information (RFIs), change orders, 
and periodic site-observation reports [29, 30].

### **2. Anatomy of an Interior Construction-Documentation (CD) Set**
A professional construction drawing set is strictly organized to clearly 
communicate technical requirements to contractors without cluttering the pages 
[22]. According to standard practices, the sheets and documents are ordered as 
follows:

**The Drawing Set** [31]
1.  **A0 Sheets (Project Information):** Cover Sheet, Accessibility Notes and 
Details, Site Plan, and Life Safety Plan.
2.  **A1 Sheets:** Demolition Plans (showing what existing interior elements 
will be removed).
3.  **A2 Sheets:** Floor Plans (the new layout of the spaces).
4.  **A3 Sheets:** Elevations and Building Sections.
5.  **A4 Sheets (Finish Plans):** Reflected Ceiling Plans (RCP), Power Plans, 
Finish Plans, and Finish Schedules.
6.  **A5 Sheets:** Interior Elevations (showing vertical details of custom 
cabinetry, tile layouts, etc.).
7.  **A6 Sheets (Schedules and Wall Types):** Door Schedules, Window Schedules,
Equipment Schedules, and Wall Schedules.
8.  **AD Sheets:** Architectural Details (zoomed-in, specific construction 
assemblies).
9.  **Consultant Sheets:** Structural (S), Mechanical (M), Electrical (E), and 
Plumbing (P) drawings, followed by any specialty consultants (e.g., acoustical 
or kitchen design).

**Specifications**
Accompanying the drawing set is the specification package. While drawings show 
*where* and *how* things are built, specifications outline the exact materials,
finishes, and products to be used, such as specific flooring types, cabinetry 
styles, and plumbing/lighting fixtures [23].

### **3. Phase-Gates Requiring Client Sign-Off**
To prevent wasted effort, costly redesigns, and scope creep, formal client 
sign-offs are required at the following critical phase-gates:

*   **End of Programming:** The client must formally sign off on the summarized
program, budget expectations, and defined scope of work before any actual 
design drafting begins [32].
*   **End of Schematic Design:** The client must approve the selected floor 
plan layout and overall design concept so the team can proceed to refine those 
specific ideas [14, 15].
*   **End of Design Development:** The client must approve the final selection 
of materials, finishes, fixtures, and the finalized layout before the team 
invests the heavy hours required to draft the technical Construction Documents 
[15, 18]. 
*   **Project Closeout (End of Construction):** After the final walk-through, 
the client reviews and agrees to the **punch list** (remaining touch-ups). Once
these are completed, the client signs the **Certificate of Substantial 
Completion**, officially concluding the project [33, 34].
