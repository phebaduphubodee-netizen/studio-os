## 1. WELT / PIPING — the line that says "two panels were sewn together"

Standard cording cores span **3 mm to 10 mm** diameter. The trade sizes:

| trade size | inch | mm |
|---|---|---|
| Size 00 | 1/8 | **3.18 mm** |
| Size 0 | 5/32 | **3.97 mm** |
| Size 1 | 3/16 | **4.76 mm** |
| Size 2 | 1/4 | **6.35 mm** |
| Size 4 | 3/8 | **9.53 mm** |
| Size 5 | 1/2 | **12.70 mm** |
| WC02232 | 22/32 | **17.46 mm** |
| WC01 / Size 7 | 1.0 | **25.40 mm** |

Sources: *Cotton Piping Cord Sizes for Arts and Crafts* plus the synthesised report.

**Which diameter goes with which furniture class is NOT IN SOURCES.** Do not
infer one; pick from the **3 mm to 10 mm** span the source calls standard — the
table's 12.70, 17.46 and 25.40 mm trade sizes sit outside that span and the DR
does not reconcile the two — and declare the choice.

**Single vs double welt is a construction law, not a style choice:**

- **Single welt** — one core wrapped in fabric, stitched into the junction of two
  intersecting panels, **forming a distinct, round, raised edge that runs along
  the seam**. The standard cording construction for loose cushions, sofas and
  armrests.
  (**Synthesised report only** — no independent trade source on this row.)
- **Double welt** — two parallel cords side by side. Applied **strictly to rigid
  furniture frames, back panels and outer chair backs where fabric is tacked
  directly to wood or plywood**, because its job is to hide the exposed staple
  line. **It is never used on loose cushions.**
  (The *Welt Cord for Piping - Upholster.com* citation attaches to the
  never-on-loose-cushions rule specifically — marker [5] — plus the synthesised
  report; the rest of this row carries the synthesised report alone.)

**Where the welt runs** (both rows: **synthesised report only** — not
independently corroborated):
- **Upholstered headboard** — **standard welt** around the **outer perimeter**,
  framing the panel and visually isolating it from the wall behind. The source
  says only "standard welt" and never defines that phrase against the
  single/double pair.
- **Boxed bench cushion** — most commonly along the **top and bottom perimeter
  edges**; it may additionally run every structural seam including the vertical
  corners of the boxing strip.

> **Read this against our own frame.** The source classifies "rigid furniture
> frames, back panels and outer chair backs" as double-welt and never classes a
> headboard either way; the DR also uses "Standard Welt Cord Diameters" as a
> generic heading over its whole trade-size table, so "standard welt" is not a
> construction-type term in this answer. OUR INFERENCE, to be tested and not
> cited: a headboard whose fabric is tacked to a rigid board matches the
> double-welt *condition*, while a bench seat cushion is a loose cushion and so
> falls squarely inside the sourced single-welt rule. One room, two different
> edge details — which is exactly the distinction a single extruded slab cannot
> express.

## 2. HEADBOARD PANEL WORK — channelled, tufted, or plain-wrapped

### 2.1 Vertical channel tufting

A standard channel is built **101.6 mm** (4 in) wide. The construction stack that
produces it — the batting and fabric are cut oversize, in the source's own words,
"to allow the fabric to wrap around the deep edges":

| part | cut to |
|---|---|
| rigid backing board and interior foam | **101.6 mm** |
| wrapping batting | **127.0 mm** (5 in) |
| cased upholstery fabric | **203.2 mm** (8 in) |

Source: *How to Make a Channel Tufted Upholstered Bench* plus the synthesised
report. **Channel width bands in millimetres are NOT IN SOURCES** — 101.6 mm is
one standard build, not a range.

**Overall headboard thickness (depth)** is commonly **76.2 mm** (3 in) or
**109.2 mm** (4.3 in). Sources are two retail product listings (*Queen Palisades
Upholstered Headboard Light Brown - Serta: Channel Tufting, Wood Frame Mounted -
Target*, *Curved Boucle Queen Bed With Smart Led Ambient Strip Sculptural Uphols*)
plus the synthesised report — retailer listings, not manufacturer specs. **The
precise depth of the vertical recesses themselves is NOT IN SOURCES.**

### 2.2 Button and diamond tufting

| pattern | spacing | note | source for this row |
|---|---|---|---|
| staggered diamond | **generally 152.4 mm to 203.2 mm** (6-8 in) | with a **101.6 mm** horizontal offset between rows — the offset is what makes it a diamond | *Tufted Leather Furniture - Timeless & Trendy*, *DIY Ottoman Coffee Table Part 1 - How To Do Diamond Tufting - Addicted 2 Decorating* + synthesised report |
| biscuit / square | **generally 8-10 in**, printed in the source as **200.0-250.0 mm** [the source's own conversion; exact would be 203.2-254.0 mm] | uniform squares or rectangles | *A Guide to Different Types of Tufting* + synthesised report |
| general headboard rows | **standard 203.2 mm** (8 in) vertical — the source states this flatly as standard, from a single DIY build source; it gives no band | first row **254.0 mm** (10 in) down from the top edge | *DIY Tufted-Upholstered Headboard* + synthesised report |

Both spacing bands are hedged **"generally"** in the source; neither is a rule.

**Button recess depth is NOT IN SOURCES.** What the synthesised report gives
instead (no trade source cited on this row) is the mechanism: the recess is
bounded by the foam core thickness — traditionally **50.8 mm** (2 in) or
**76.2 mm** (3 in) — and the button is pulled with twine until it **bottoms out
against the wooden backing board**. So the recess is constrained by the core, not
chosen freely.

### 2.3 Plain wrapped panels — the modular-reveal case

For wall-integrated headboards, the high-end modern residential standard is
**rectangular or square panels upholstered individually and mounted side by side
with consistent reveals of 1/8 inch to 3/16 inch (3.18 mm to 4.76 mm)**.
**Synthesised report ONLY — no trade source corroborates this reveal band**, and
it is the one dimensional figure in §2.3, so a consumer taking "panel reveals"
from this file is taking an uncorroborated number.

**Edge radius produced by wrapping foam over a panel edge is NOT IN SOURCES.**

**Fabric tension** (**synthesised report only** — not independently corroborated):
pulled taut but not over-tightened — no unwanted creases or folds, and the foam
core must not distort or buckle. Some designs deliberately allow **slight, soft
ripples or folds** to emphasise material softness. There is no number for either
state.

> **Why the three faces above matter for a render.** §2.1, §2.2 and §2.3 are
> three different faces. A headboard that is one flat plane with a seam down its
> centre is none of the three the source enumerates — and the source names no
> fourth, but it also never says the list is exhaustive and nowhere discusses a
> centre-split face. So: a centre split with no reveal and no welt has no assembly
> story we can point at in these sources — **OUR reading, not a sourced
> prohibition on what the trade builds.**

## 3. BOXING AND BORDERS — side height, corners and closures

The source gives each class as a metric figure (a metric band for the daybed
class) **or** an imperial band, joined by "or" — two alternative statements, NOT
a conversion of one another. They are carried that way here, so nobody derives
50.8 mm or 76.2 mm from the inch half believing it is the same figure:

| class | boxing height |
|---|---|
| slimline seating | **50.0 mm** (source also gives the band as 1-2 in) |
| standard bench seating | **75.0 mm** (source also gives the band as 2-3 in) |
| deep seating / lounge | **100.0 mm** (source also gives the band as 3-4 in) |
| daybed | **120.0 mm to 150.0 mm** (source also gives the band as 4+ in) |

Sources: *How to Measure Outdoor Lounge Cushions Australia*, *How Thick Should a
Bench Cushion Be? - Rulaer* plus the synthesised report.

**Top-stitch / flange offset from the seam is NOT IN SOURCES** for seat cushions.
The only adjacent figure the sources give is a standard double-hem topstitch on
canvas at **6.35 mm** (1/4 in) (*Sewing Archives - Storefront Life* plus the
synthesised report) — a different operation on a different material.

### 3.1 Corners

- **Seamed and clipped** — the standard: seamed corners with **rolled edges**.
  Interior seam allowances are clipped at 45 degrees leaving exactly
  **6.35 mm** (1/4 in) of fabric from the stitch line so the corner lies flat.
- **Mitred** — a precision technique for sharp 90-degree corners in double hems
  on heavy material, reducing the corner from nine overlapping fabric layers to
  three. Also used for corner or L-shaped bench cushions that must join cleanly
  without a gap.
- **Gathered** — reserved for bolster pillows and closed with pull cords.
  **Not used on flat rectangular bench cushions.**

Sources, per corner type: seamed and clipped — *Stitch & Zip: How to Sew a Bench
Cushion Cover with a Zipper - Rulaer*; mitred — *How to Sew Mitered Corners — 2
Ways - Sailrite*, *RHF Straight Mitred Corner Back Cushion Cover: Custom Fit*,
*Mitred Cushion Covers Made to Measure | Bench & Corner Boat Cushions UK - The
Foam Shop*; gathered — *A BOLSTER PILLOW & PILLOW SHAMS*. Each plus the
synthesised report.

### 3.2 Zipper / closure placement

| placement | consequence |
|---|---|
| **back boxing** (horizontal along the centre of the rear boxing strip, wrapping the back corners by several inches) | cushion stays **flippable**; zipper tape cut **50.8 mm** (2 in) shorter than the overall cushion width |
| **underside** (into the bottom plate) | cording and hardware hidden, cushion **non-flippable** — **synthesised report only, not independently corroborated** |
| **bottom edge** (loose back cushions) | hides where the backrest meets the seat cushion |
| **around the middle** (three sides at mid-thickness) | thick boxed backrests, equilateral bolsters |
| **envelope** (overlapping panels, no zipper) | primarily decorative pillows |

Sources, per row (the source attributes each closure separately — they do not
pool): back boxing — *Which Cushion Type Is Right for Me? - Sailrite* and *bench
cushion | Do-It-Yourself Advice Blog.*; bottom edge and around the middle —
*Custom Replacement Back Cushions for Seats & Sofas | FoamOrder*; envelope —
*Outdoor Cushion Terminology Guide*; **underside — the synthesised report ONLY**.
The synthesised report co-signs every row.

## 4. SEAM PLACEMENT AND ALLOWANCE

- Primary structural seams **MUST align precisely with the outer edges** where the
  top/bottom plates meet the boxing, mirroring the foam's own shape.
- Vertical seams joining multiple boxing strips are **split and placed
  symmetrically along the left and right sides**, so no raw seam or fabric joint
  disrupts the front-facing profile of the bench.
- Cording or welt joins are **hidden along the lower rear run**, keeping the
  thick overlapped junction out of direct view.

Sources for the three placement rules above: *How to Measure Couch Cushions for
Replacement Foam*, *What Is Furniture Contrast Welt? A Homeowner's Guide* plus the
synthesised report. (The source also cites a r/upholstery Reddit thread here;
dropped as low-tier.)

**Seam allowance:**
- **standard** cased cording joins use **12.70 mm** (1/2 in)
- modern interior covers frequently specify a narrower **9.53 mm** (3/8 in),
  favoured because it minimises bulk and prevents a visible raised fabric bump or
  shadow line from showing behind the seam on the exterior face

Sources for the seam-allowance bullets only: *Seam finishing for cushions and
covers - Marine Fabricator*, *documentation - Fabric Calculator by Sailrite* plus
the synthesised report. (A second r/upholstery Reddit thread is also cited here;
dropped as low-tier.)

> **The allowance may be a RENDER fact, not only a sewing fact — but the source
> does not say so.** The 9.53 mm allowance is chosen because it *prevents* a
> raised bump or shadow line from showing behind the seam. The source never states
> that the 12.70 mm allowance does show one, and gives no threshold at which it
> appears; it explicitly refuses cue visibility at 2-4 m (§7). Whether the wider
> allowance's bump shows at OUR camera is ours to measure, and its absence would
> be a cue too.

**Radiating pull lines around tufts and seams — count and amplitude are NOT IN
SOURCES.** (The source files this under seam placement, which is why it sits
here.) The qualitative law is usable, and it is the opposite of randomness:
in professional diamond tufting the craftsman **deliberately manipulates the
excess fabric allowance between buttons so that all resulting pleats fold
consistently in one direction**, creating uniform structured shadows. **Crooked,
heavy or lumpy pleats are a structural failure** caused by inadequate fabric
allowance, shallow button pulling, or weak interior padding support.

Sources for the pull-lines row only: *Tufted Upholstery Guide: Styles,
Techniques & Ideas | Beautiful Homes*, *Deep Buttoning and Diamond Planning |
Upholstery Handbook* plus the synthesised report.

## 5. FOAM AND COMPRESSION — what has a formula and what has only a test

**Crown (top-surface convexity) in mm per metre is NOT IN SOURCES.** A standard
heavy-wrap profiling formula the sources do quantify (one build, not a range):

- a **101.6 mm** (4 in) raw foam core, wrapped with a **38.1 mm** (1.5 in) Dacron
  fibrefill sheet on both the top and bottom faces, yields a finished cushion with
  approximately **177.8 mm** (7 in) of centre loft while the outer border/boxing
  remains constrained to **101.6 mm** — a centre rise of **76.2 mm** (3 in) over
  the border height.
- a standard **25.4 mm** (1 in) uncompressed Dacron wrap compresses to
  approximately **8.47 mm** (1/3 in) under the cover.

Sources: *How to Measure Couch Cushions for Replacement Foam*, *Custom Cut
Upholstery Foam - Fabric Warehouse* plus the synthesised report.

**Dent depth under load per fill class is NOT IN SOURCES, and the reason is
structural: the trade quantifies FORCE, not displacement.** Indentation Load
Deflection (ILD, also IFD) measures the force in pounds required to compress a
**609.6 mm x 609.6 mm x 101.6 mm** foam block by **25.4 mm** (25% of its
thickness) using a 50-square-inch circular indenter foot.
(Sources for the ILD test definition: *Foam | Unlock The Mystery - Kim's
Upholstery*, *Marine Foam Cushions, RV Cushions, Mattress Foam, Foam Inserts*
plus the synthesised report.)

| class | ILD |
|---|---|
| ultra-soft | 10 to 25 lb |
| medium to medium-soft | 28 to 40 lb |
| firm | 40 to 45 lb |
| extra-firm | 45 to 55+ lb |

Sources for the band table only: *Custom Cut Upholstery Foam - Fabric Warehouse*,
*CushionCraft Upholstery Foam*, *Boat Upholstery, Boat Seats & Cushions*, *Seat
Depth and Cushion Firmness Guide for Better Lounging - chita living*, *Upholstery
Foam Sheets (By Inches) | FoamOrder* plus the synthesised report.

> **Therefore "how far does a cushion dent when someone sits on it" is, for now, a
> SOLVER question for us (SoftBody, or a measured photograph), not a lookup.**
> These 153 sources do not carry it, and what they carry instead is a force test —
> that is a fact about this corpus, not about the whole literature. This is the
> same shape as the inspection-blindness finding of 2026-08-08: a refusal that
> converts a research question into a local experiment.

**Foam edge radius on wrapped panels is NOT IN SOURCES.** Two adjacent numbers
appear in the same answer and are NOT the answer — do not borrow either: wood
frame edges are beveled and broken **to prevent fabric wear**, and the standard
radius planes used for that cut a **3 mm** radius or **4 mm** bevel/rounding in
wood — the figures belong to the tool, not to a stated frame-edge practice — and
in **material-performance testing** indenter edges are calibrated to a **1 mm**
radius. The source attaches the 1 mm figure to material performance testing
generally, NOT to the ILD indenter — its own ILD indenter is a 50-square-inch
circular foot with no radius given. The DR pools *Edge and bevel plane - DICTUM*
and *ISO 3385: fatigue by constant-load pounding test on soft foam | ZwickRoell*
(a constant-load pounding fatigue test, a different test from ILD/IFD) across
BOTH figures rather than assigning one title to each.

## 6. THE MODELED-vs-MAPPED SPLIT — the actionable law of this whole file

This is the table that tells a build where each cue must live. It is a five-way
split, not a preference.

| cue | where it lives |
|---|---|
| micro-weave, fibre detail | **texture only** — a **Tangent-Space Normal Map**, RGB encoding thread direction and micro-shadow at pixel level with no added polygons |
| major creases, wrinkles, folds | **physically modeled** in mesh geometry, using diagonal and curved cuts |
| edge bevels and chamfers | **physically modeled** in mesh geometry — every upholstered edge, cushion boundary and seam frame |
| button / diamond indentations | **physically modeled** in mesh geometry, with a **Baked Ambient Occlusion (AO) Map** reinforcing the contact shadow and crevice shading |
| specularity and glossiness | **texture only** — a Roughness Map |

Three consequences, in the DR ANSWER's own words (marker [29], which the DR
attributes collectively to its six §6 titles plus its own synthesised report,
seven citations in all — no individual source is quoted verbatim anywhere in
the DR):

- *"relying purely on normal maps for large folds results in a flat, artificial
  look under raking light"* — which is why a mapped-only headboard can pass under
  flat light and fail the moment a key light rakes across it.
- *"infinitely sharp 90-degree polygonal edges break the soft look"* — a wrapped
  soft object with a hard mesh edge reads as a box no matter what the map does.
- *"Major creases must be modeled using diagonal and curved cuts to avoid
  straight, parallel “comb” patterns"* — which is the REASON for the diagonal
  and curved cuts the table above already requires.

Sources, pooled by the DR across the whole of §6 (all seven, none assignable to a
single row): *Blender 2.8 Sofa Wrinkles and Creases — Pro Modeling Guide Part 2 |
iMeshh*, *Creating Seamless PBR Textures for Realistic 3D Fabric Patterns and
Embroidery*, *FAQ - Superellipse*, *Free 3D textures: Where to download and how to
use them - Chaos Blog*, *Creating Realistic Furniture Plastic Textures for PBR
Workflows*, *What is a roughness map? Definition, use & tips* plus the synthesised
report.
