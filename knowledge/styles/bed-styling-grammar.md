## 1. The layer stack, bottom to top (hotel SOP + staging practice)

mattress pad (concealed) → fitted sheet → flat sheet laid FACE-DOWN, top edge
at the mattress head → warmth layer (blanket/duvet) set ~150 mm lower than the
sheet's top edge → (triple-sheeting: third flat sheet over the blanket, folded
back together with the second) → bedspread/comforter → decorative runner/throw
across the bottom third. Residential staging variant: skirt → fitted → flat
face-down → blanket leaving ~300 mm at the head → comforter folded in thirds
across the foot → pillows → throw.

## 2. Fold-back at the head — every sourced band

- Flat sheet folded back over the duvet: ~305 mm from the headboard [Hotel
  Bedding Layers Explained; hotel SOP].
- Traditional visible cuff of sheet+blanket: 150–205 mm [Hotel Housekeeping
  Bed Making Procedure SOP].
- Triple-sheeting double-fold: ~150 mm encapsulating the blanket edge
  [1Concier triple-sheeting method].
- Blanket-top-to-headboard space: ~300–457 mm; one procedure folds exactly
  one-quarter of the bed length down from the top [bedmaking-procedure].
- Turndown (evening) peels the top layers at a 45-degree angle on the
  nightstand side [Hotel Housekeeping SOPs 2026]; daytime-made smooths the
  spread over the pillows to the headboard and tucks under their front edge.

## 3. Side drop — the numbers behind "no bare flank"

Drop measured from mattress top downward:

| layer class | drop |
|---|---|
| coverlet | 8 to 12 inches (203.2 to 304.8 mm) [Quilt Sizes Chart, Stitchin' Heaven] |
| comforter | 12 to 16 inches (304.8 to 406.4 mm) [same] |
| bedspread | 457–635 mm (to near floor) [same] |
| bed skirt / valance | 356 or 381 mm [Or & Zon; staging checklist] |
| throw/runner | 300–450 mm [Throw Blanket Size Guide] |

Styling default when staging: 406–457 mm side drop, 381–406 mm foot drop
[QuiltKeeper drops & overhangs chart]; drop scales with mattress class
(innerspring 254–305 mm … box-spring stacks 457–610 mm).

**Bare mattress sides are never acceptable in professional staging or
hospitality photography** — concealed by skirt, wrap, or a low-draping
duvet/coverlet. This is the trade's own wording of what our sighted panel
measured (delivered work 16/18 "none", 2 "sliver"): the flank ratchet in
`bed_pixels.py` is enforcing a real industry norm, not a house preference.

## 4. Pillow staging (Matouk formations chart)

- Queen four-layer: 2 euro shams (27" x 27", 1" flange) → 2 sleeping pillows →
  2 standard shams (21" x 27") → 2 boudoir (12" x 16").
- King layered trio: 3 euro shams → 2 king pillows (50 cm x 90 cm) in shams →
  3 boudoir; symmetrical and gap-filling variants in the unit.
- Hotel counts: 5-star 4–6 pillows mixed; 3-star 2. Holiday Inn manual: firm
  king fill min 1700g, casing 100% cotton ≥233 TPI double-stitched.
- Karate-chop is CONTESTED in the trade (for: catches highlights, signals
  fresh; against: "1982", contrived on patterned covers) — treat as a style
  choice to read off the anchor pool, never a default.
- Lean angle, neighbour overlap mm, rear-rank visible height: NOT IN SOURCES —
  measure from the anchor pool (R4), do not invent.

## 5. Surface finish — what research refuses to number

Wrinkle amplitude/wavelength on a made duvet vs flat sheet: NOT IN SOURCES;
whether stylists press flat then re-crease: NOT IN SOURCES. The only sourced
handle is thin-film buckling (λ = 2πh(Ē_f/3Ē_s)^(1/3)) — a modelling lever,
not a target. The appearance numbers for THIS studio therefore come from the
anchor pool by measurement, which stays the R4 law; this file deliberately
does not fill them.

## 6. Brand-spec anchors (sizes a bought asset can be checked against)

Hilton linen sizing [Hotel Linen Weight and Size Standards]: double duvet
250 x 250 cm in a 252 x 252 cover over a 203 x 176 mattress — i.e. the duvet is
authored ~40% wider than its mattress; flat sheets 290 x 290. Single duvet
250 x 190 over 203 x 121. These corroborate, from the supply side, why a duvet
whose extent cannot span the mattress plus a real drop (our D-101 case: 1438 mm
over a 1969 mm mattress) can never read as made.

### 8.1 THE AXIS THIS FILE WAS MISSING: which layer is allowed to wrinkle

> *"with my duvet and my quilts and even my pillows I like to place them in a way that feels
> pretty **structured**, but when I place my throw at the end of the bed **I want some wrinkle
> and I want it to feel relaxed**, because it's that last finishing layer that makes your bed
> — and therefore your room — feel like you want to live there instead of it feeling too
> stiff."*

**The wrinkle is assigned to ONE layer on purpose; every other layer is placed structured.**

Consequence for a measured build: a duvet carrying several times the fold energy of a
delivered comforter is not a mis-tuned amount, it is **a structured layer behaving as a
relaxed one** — a class error, not a parameter error. The correct target is bracketed by two
observed extremes:

| state | what it looks like | source |
|---|---|---|
| floor | hospitality linen steamed for a camera — the top plane is essentially **planar**, the only structure is a hem crease | `cmYLQo6zZZE` frames |
| target | **structured with volume**, wrinkle assigned to the throw alone | `QF8hplMHZ2Y` |
| failure | *"just a coverlet… you need to know that it's going to **look very flat** on your bed"* | `QF8hplMHZ2Y` |

Both faults are named: **flat**, and **crumpled**. The aim sits between them and is not the
midpoint — it is structure plus loft.

### 8.2 CREASES ARE REMOVED FIRST, THEN SOFTNESS IS PUT BACK
New bedding goes **in the dryer** *"to fluff and get rid of the excess creases"*, and the
sewn-in tags are cut off, before any styling begins (`bvX7qZg6xqI`). Hospitality prep is the
same act with a steamer. **A build that starts from crumpled cloth and tries to tidy it is
running the process backwards.**

### 8.3 FRACTIONS — and a cross-check against §2
- **Fold-back at ~1/3 of the bed length from the head**, with the sheet cuff turned back OVER
  the coverlet (`bvX7qZg6xqI`).
  **Cross-check:** §2 already records *"one procedure folds exactly one-quarter of the bed
  length down from the top"*. So the sourced band is **1/4 to 1/3 of bed length**, and the
  two sources disagree by a quarter of that distance — record the range, do not pick one.
- **A folded quilt covers the LOWER HALF and stops just short of the mattress edge**, doubled
  back, *"making for a great tailored look"* (`bvX7qZg6xqI`).
- **Alternative look**: quilt brought up to *"a quarter of the way from the top"*, folded, and
  the coverlet laid over.
- **Duvet-at-the-foot look**: duvet folded in half, placed evenly at the foot, then one more
  fold.
- **The double fold-back**: *"you can just do a fold in half, but I prefer to **fold back and
  then back again**, so that I get this **extra fluffy band** across the bed"* (`QF8hplMHZ2Y`).
  This is buildable box-and-fold geometry, not a simulation outcome.

### 8.4 COVERAGE HAS AN UPPER BOUND TOO
> *"when I am doing my duvet covers I'm going to pull it so you get **the reveal of the bed
> frame**, and then you get to see kind of some **drape around the edges**."* (`QF8hplMHZ2Y`)

The duvet is deliberately NOT taken to full coverage — the frame is meant to show. Read with
§3's drop table: the drop bands say how far the cloth falls; this says the frame below it
must still be visible.

**And the sizing arithmetic that converts a drop into a plan coverage** (`kcefHTeuXNE`, staged
in `knowledge/_inbox/video-study/2026-08-28-rug-and-bedcover-numbers-1kItQdeo9oc.md`):

- cover **width** = mattress width **+ 2 x drape**
- cover **length** = mattress length **+ 1 x drape** (foot only — **no drape at the head**)
- drape >= mattress thickness (**102 mm foam to 254 mm+ with a topper**)
- **the top ~508 mm (20 in) of the cover sits UNDER the pillows**

That last constant is the one a plan-coverage measurement needs and a catalogue never states.

### 8.5 A STATED MINIMUM FOR "FINISHED", AND PILLOW COUNTS
> *"once you finish things off with **a sham, a quilt and a duvet** you've got nice sheeting.
> It's a very simple look **but it IS finished.** But these next steps are the layers that add
> a bit more interest, and if you're going for that look of things that feel a bit more
> **collected**, we add the decorative pillows and then a throw at the end."* (`QF8hplMHZ2Y`)

**Finished is a count of layer TYPES, not of objects** — the only threshold for "finished"
found in the whole video study. Check a bed against the layer-type list before tuning any
existing layer; the answer may be that a whole type is absent.

Counts (`QF8hplMHZ2Y`): *"I tend to like **odd groupings, so three or five pillows**"*; the
maximal decorative set built on camera is *"two big, two medium, and then one lumbar"*; shams
are sized to the bed (standard for full/queen, king for king) *"so it fills the length of the
bed"*. And the permission to subtract: a very modern interior *"might consider either
foregoing the throw."*

### 8.6 ORIENTATION
> *"I even **face the pillows in the direction of the door**. This creates a great first
> impression."* (`bvX7qZg6xqI`)

Pillow facing is keyed to **where a person arrives from**, not to the camera. It belongs with
the gaze/facing rules in `knowledge/rendering/practitioner-consensus-eye-light-camera.md`, and
it means a spec that names a camera but never names the door is missing the datum this rule
needs.
