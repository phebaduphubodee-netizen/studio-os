## 1. Surface vignettes (nightstand · dresser · bench · shelf bay)

- **Odd rule with surface mapping [CITED — MontCarta "3-5-7 Rule", Tidbits & Twine]:**
  3 items = constrained surfaces (nightstands, small pedestals, compact shelf bays) ·
  5 = medium (dressers, credenzas, coffee tables, mantels) · 7 = expansive (long tables, multi-bay
  shelving). Escape valve when a grouping feels heavy: *remove the smallest item*.
- **Symmetry legitimately overrides** the odd rule for: flanking pairs at the bed (lamps/nightstands),
  formal/traditional and Chinese/Peranakan registers, strict minimalism (single statement piece).
  Real-estate staging PREFERS symmetric bedsides [ask-04 §4, Emily Henderson reader thread].
- **Decorative triangle [CITED — MontCarta, Creek Line House, Kylie M]:** tallest at back = anchor;
  second-largest beside it; smallest in front. Heights must vary — a flat skyline reads unresolved.
  - Medium element ≈ **½–⅔ of the tall element** [CITED — MontCarta states "half to two-thirds"].
  - Low element = "a flat or very short item" that grounds the group (book stack, tray, stone)
    [CITED as words]; the 0.15–0.25× ratio is **[SYNTH]** — our own translation to a number.
- **Depth layering [1SRC — CB2, order-of-operations]:** place largest pieces first (X-pattern across a
  grid), fill with medium, add small last. Rear = tall/flat leaned pieces; front = trays/low platforms
  carrying small detailed objects [SYNTH structure; the CB2 sequence is the cited part].
- **Contrast footprints:** never two identical-height / identical-base neighbours — wide round beside
  thin angular beside rectilinear stack [CITED as principle across MontCarta/Tip Top].
- **How full may a surface be:** occupied ≈ **40% of the surface, 60% exposed** [1SRC — Roomtodo
  "60/40 rule", the only independent quantified line]. The per-surface lived-in/staged vacancy table
  (nightstand 30–40/60–70% …) is **[SYNTH]** — use as bracketing start values only.

## 2. Wall art — sizing & hanging [CITED ×3 each unless noted]

- Width = **⅔–¾ of the furniture below** (headboard/console); never wider than the furniture.
  Art on a bare wall: 40–60% of active wall width [SYNTH-leaning; main answer only].
- Centre of art at **57–60″ = 1448–1524 mm** from floor (bare walls, museum standard);
  seated zones drop to 40–44″ = 1016–1118 mm [1SRC].
- Bottom edge **6–10″ = 152–254 mm above the headboard**; above dresser 6–8″ = 152–203 mm
  (4–6″ if tall surface objects overlap the art) [1SRC for the overlap variant].
- Multi-piece sets: uniform gaps — 2–3″ (51–76 mm) small frames · 4–5″ (102–127 mm) large;
  gap >6″ (152 mm) breaks the set into separate pieces [SYNTH-leaning; not re-verified].
- **Sources split (declared open):** headboards >48″ tall — skip art vs thin panoramic near ceiling.
- Bedroom-register subject/frame bounds → §6 matrix (calm horizontals; busy geometric abstracts
  prohibited over the bed [1SRC]).

## 3. Bedroom rugs [CITED ×3–4 each]

- Extend **18–24″ = 457–610 mm beyond bed sides and foot**. <18″ reads skimpy/accidental;
  >30″ (762 mm) overwhelms a small room.
- Keep **12–18″ = 305–457 mm bare floor to walls**; compact urban rooms may drop to ~8″ = 203 mm.
- Three layouts: **fully-under** (nightstands + bench on the rug; king → 9×12–10×14 ft, queen →
  8×10–9×12 ft) · **two-thirds-under** (most common; rug starts 6–12″ = 152–305 mm in front of the
  nightstands, nightstands on bare floor) · **runners** (2×6/2×8 ft flanking; wider than the
  nightstand, never past the foot).
- **Foot bench changes the rule:** rug runs ≥12″ = 305 mm past the bench front [1SRC states 12″;
  a second source confirms direction only]; luxury register 24–36″. Bench all four legs on the rug
  [1SRC — Aosom]. Can't reach it → size the rug up a class, don't slide it.
- **Contested (both camps documented, neither wins):** nightstands ON the rug (hotel island) vs OFF
  (two-thirds camp). Our spec's asymmetric two-thirds-style rug is inside documented practice.

## 4. Whole-room completeness — there is NO cited total count

**No independent source states a room-wide object count** (asked directly; unit `ask-06`). Any
whole-room threshold (e.g. "≥12 loose objects") is a house number, not literature. Sources are
per-zone; a complete styled bedroom =

| zone | staged / restrained | editorial / lived-warm |
|---|---|---|
| bed pillows | 2–4 | 5–6 (2/person sleep + 3 euro + optional lumbar) [CITED] |
| bedding layers | 3 | up to 5 incl. foot throw [CITED] |
| each nightstand | 2–3 ("lamp, book, glass") | 4 [CITED] |
| dresser top | 3 (tray · mirror · few frames) | 5–6 (lamp, tray, frames, tall vase w/ branches) [1SRC ea.] |
| seating nook | chair + lamp + table, +1 cushion +1 throw [1SRC formula] | same, throw pooling |
| floor | 1 rug (§3) | may layer/offset |
| walls | 1 statement piece or 2–3-piece set | same, more personal |
| windows | sheer + blackout double layer [1SRC] | same |
| plants | 1 | 2 (one trailing high, one sculptural mid) [SYNTH counts; species CITED] |

- Density fails BOTH ways, quotably: bare shelves/empty tables = "challenge ANY interior to sing"
  (sterile), and the Emily Henderson over-styling reversal ("liked it more when it was WAYY more
  paired back") = clutter. Keep surfaces "clean but not empty".

## 5. Open-shelf grid grammar (the "empty shelves / staged bays" answer)

- **≥25% of every shelf surface empty**; airy-minimal register: books ≤ ⅓ of shelf space, one
  significant object per section [1SRC — Tip Top, concrete ratios].
- **X-pattern / diagonal distribution [CITED — CB2, Creek Line House]:** stagger same-size/shape/
  colour pieces diagonally across the grid, never stacked in the same column; largest placed first.
- **Identical-item bounds [1SRC — Creek Line House]:** 2 identical = bold/modern · 3-in-a-row =
  peaceful/quaint · **>3 = retail merchandising, prohibited.** (Consistent with our max-2-instances
  per asset id in frame — ORD-25c amendment already stricter.)

## 6. Story → object list (ORD-2026-08-25c steps 1–3, now grounded)

Five DOCUMENTED methods (verbatim quotes in unit `ask-04`):
1. **Script breakdown** (Peerspace, film set dressing): scene-by-scene notes → itemized dressing list;
   socioeconomics + interests of the character pick material quality and subjects; period/geography research.
2. **Props list + runsheet** (MIT Theater Guild): rough list from the script → director alignment →
   prop/set/costume boundary talk → runsheet mapping item↔scene↔character↔status → cull ~25% on
   condition/durability/reusability.
3. **E-commerce room-set storytelling** (Prodoto): choose characters → style follows character →
   plant story cues (open book, cup on side table) → mood-board-aligned colour scheme.
4. **Persona-driven FF&E kits** (Ark & Mason, Airbnb tier): 2–3 persona profiles → priorities →
   spec sheets with SKUs + sourcing links; stage for the 3 best camera angles, then strip what crowds frame.
5. **Mood-board motif analysis** (Kevin Francis): 30–50 reference images → recurring motifs (colour
   temp, era, texture, scale) → name ONE anchor-object archetype → test in mock layout.
- **ABSENT from literature (cited absence):** which bed side belongs to which resident → stays a
  story decision we author. Natural-asymmetry devices that ARE documented: nightstands harmonious in
  style/scale/finish rather than identical; align art bottom edges even over unequal tables; round rug
  under one bed corner for asymmetric layouts.

## 7. Style membership — warm contemporary tropical minimal (STY-2 criteria content)

Per-class CHECKABLE attributes (verbatim support per class in unit `ask-05`; sources = Singapore
Contemporary / Tropical Modern / Japandi-adjacent guides):

| class | form | materials | finishes | colour bounds | prohibited |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| table lamp | soft organic sculptural, curved | ceramic, clay, stone, unlacquered brass, wood, rattan | matte/textured; brushed brass, matte black | sand, warm white, cream, black, brass | chrome, polished stainless, high-gloss lacquer, cool/harsh light |
| vase/vessel | fluid rounded organic | hand-thrown pottery, raw ceramic, stoneware, handblown glass, terracotta, cement | tactile matte/rough/weathered | sand, warm white, terracotta, clay, muted olive | glossy industrial, factory-perfect glass/plastic |
| wall art | calm horizontals; line art, botanicals, soft landscapes, ink-wash | canvas/paper; light oak or thin matte black/white frames | matte, non-reflective, thin profiles | muted/monochrome, warm beige, slate, sage | busy geometric abstracts (bedroom), saturated primaries, ornate frames, heavy glass over headboard |
| books | hardback; mixed horiz+vert stacks as platforms | linen/leather/matte-cloth bindings | tactile matte | like-coloured neutral spines | glossy jackets, neon spines |
| tray/bowl | simple low circular/rect | teak, acacia, bamboo, travertine, stone, clay, matte brass | natural grain, unpolished, unglazed | teak brown, sand, brass, slate, matte black | gloss plastic, ornate patterned porcelain |
| plant+pot | restrained sculptural silhouettes; trailing on shelves | pot: aged terracotta, raw clay, unglazed stoneware, cement, wood | raw/aged/rough | off-white, sand, terracotta, stone grey, charcoal | jungle-scale species, plastic planters, high-gloss glazes |
| throw/cushion | layered edit of scales (large square + lumbar) | linen, cotton, wool, bouclé, felt, cashmere | visible weave/slub; breathable | sand, taupe, cream, greige, sage, charcoal, soft terracotta | high-shine polyester, loud patterns/contrast |
| personal objects | simple honest functional | clear glass, handmade ceramic, raw stone, wood | handcrafted, slightly weathered ok | warm earth + glass + soft brass | mass plastic, neon cords, retail labels |

- **Plant size classes [1SRC bands, usable with citation]:** floor 900–1500+ mm tall / pot Ø300–450 mm ·
  tabletop 300–600 / Ø150–250 · shelf-trailing 150–300 / Ø100–150.
- **Cushion sizes on seating [1SRC]:** armchair = one 550×550 statement OR large square + 300×500
  lumbar; bench = 2 cushions on ONE side, remaining ⅔ bare or throw-draped; throw covers ⅓–½ of a
  bench, diagonal, folded lengthwise in thirds; drape = hold centre, let fall, scrunch — never pressed.
  Throw/cushion sits several shades off the upholstery; contrast texture (smooth chair ↔ rough throw).
- **Library-filtering workflows [each 1SRC]:** 80/20 style blending (80% in-style, 20% personality
  accents — bounds how many off-style pieces may enter) · 70/20/10 colour proportioning ·
  mood-board motif analysis (§6.5) · prop-vetting cull questions (condition / durability / reusability).
