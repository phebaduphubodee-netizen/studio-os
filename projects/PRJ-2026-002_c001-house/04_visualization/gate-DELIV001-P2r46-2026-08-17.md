**Why nothing on this tier reaches the bar, stated as geometry rather than as a
mood.** Fineness is measured in WORLD space, so a fine small mesh scaled up to
cover a bed is a coarse mesh. This tier's dense meshes are all **sub-metre
objects**: `pillow_set_a` is 63,492 triangles on a ~600 mm pillow (10.3 mm);
`799b6961`'s dense parts are 88,390 triangles on **1089 mm** objects (9.55 mm). Its
parts that are actually cover-sized (1342 x 2250 mm) carry 4,028 triangles and
measure **26.4 mm**. A ~2 m cover at 10.3 mm needs about **75,408 triangles in one
sheet**; SketchUp models are budgeted per object and spend that on a hero chair,
not on a duvet.

### The mattress — the work I announced, refuted by our own file

`value_ladder.py` says `bed__mattress` is **0.34% of the frame** and "almost
entirely occluded by the coverlet it lies under", and deliberately does not rank
it. It renders today at **12.55% (542,175 px) — 37x its designed share** — at
0.851 of frame p99 against delivered work's 0.505-0.674 bedding band. Its signed
identity is a **sheeted** mattress (`value_ladder` rung `sheet`, material
`linen_white`), so giving it the tape edge and quilting of a BARE mattress would
fabricate a reading (R10). **It is not under-built; it is over-exposed.** The fix
for 12.55% is the cloth, not the box. Recorded as D-092, with the contingency
named: if he wants the frame shot bare, the mattress becomes the work that hour,
