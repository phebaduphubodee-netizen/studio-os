## Three lines

1. **"The free tier is exhausted" was wrong twice over, and this round found out
   why.** The set that dresses the bed — `d4698c95` "BED SETS", 621 downloads — was
   on the search list at p2r46 and was skipped by a byte ceiling that logged it as
   *"deferred, not judged"*. It could not have been judged anyway: **the SketchUp
   GLTF Exporter writes ONE PRIMITIVE PER TRIANGLE**, so `mesh_density` read its
   133,108-triangle duvet as a **1-triangle sheet at 2785 mm** — the coarsest
   possible reading of the finest cloth on the shelf — and Blender needed hours to
   import 297,640 primitives. Both are fixed (`objects()` aggregates per mesh;
   `glb_flatten.py` merges per material, 360 MB → 35.5 MB, triangle count asserted
   identical in and out). A third instrument was refusing sets before either could
   look: the staging fit was solved on the **file's** bounding box, so any bedding
   set shipping with the bed it dresses was refused for the BED's height — six of
   the nine size-refusals on this shelf.
2. **The bet, written before the render, and lost.** D-093 let the cover miss the
   fineness cut by 4.1% (10.69 mm against a 10.27 mm control) under an exception
   bounded by name, by number and by printing. Its refutation clause said: *refuted
   if either independent critic files the bed cover as coarse, faceted or plastic.*
   **C3/Gemini: "ขอบผ้าคมเกินไปเหมือนพลาสติก" — the cloth's edge is too sharp, like
   plastic. Both critics ranked the bedding #1.** The exception is recorded as
   LOST. What the loss does **not** show is that 10.7 mm is too coarse: both critics
   filed the same defect against the acquired **pillows**, which measure 4.5 and
   10.3 mm and **are this cut's control** — C2 filed it in as many words, *"One
   systemic error, not four"*, across pillows, folded throw and hung garments. A cut
   whose own control draws the complaint is not the lever for the complaint.
3. **The eye found what 94% coverage cannot see.** C2's first item: *"The bed is
   only half made… reads as a fitted mattress protector."* Both are true. The ray
   counts cloth-above-mattress and the set carries a flat 1734 × 2017 mm spread that
   covers the whole plan plus a 2144 × 1438 mm duvet that lies across half of it. A
   metric that cannot tell a duvet from a fitted sheet is this repo's own named
   defect — *a metric that cannot separate two things it is being asked to decide
   between* — and this time the eye found it, not the instrument (D-095).

### What changed in the rules, and what each change cost

| rung | before | after | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mesh_density` unit | densest **primitive** | densest **object** | a per-triangle export scored 1 triangle |
| glb import | 297,640 primitives, hours | 34 primitives, seconds | `glb_flatten`, 27 distinct materials behind 297,632 entries |
| staging fit | solved on the **file** bbox | solved on the **cover part** | six sets refused for the bed's height |
| `size_ok` | a **cut** at 100% of the mattress bbox | a **reported number** | it refused five candidates at 1.011x–1.125x, none ever rayed; `coverage` and `fall_sides` ask the same question by ray at a stated tolerance. `max_scale` untouched — nothing is stretched |
| set's own pillows | shipped on top of ours | dropped | `duplicate_of_placed` (volume overlap with an already-acquired object) and `lies_on_the_bed` (an EXTRA above the declared 320 mm loft is not a runner) — both geometric, no names |
| build fineness cut | **could not run, by construction** | settled on the finished scene | its control (`bench__acq`, `bed__headset0__acq`) is placed AFTER the bed, so the cut had been returning "could not run" every build since it was written |

### The tonal ladder, which failed the first full render and is the reason there were two

`bed__coverlet` resolved to two acquired meshes wearing one material — *"one rung
cannot rank two objects"*, and that refusal was right. The build's own words already
said which is which (*the spread that covers the bed IS the coverlet rung; a band
folded over it IS the duvet rung*), so the largest-plan field part is now the spread
and the rest are bands. The ladder then measured: coverlet 185.3 vs target 142.0,
pillowcase 209.4 vs 192.0, bench 86.1 vs 131.0 — all acquired rungs, **reported not
enforced**, order and span still are.
