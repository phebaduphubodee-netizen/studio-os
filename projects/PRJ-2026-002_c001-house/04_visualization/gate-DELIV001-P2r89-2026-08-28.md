## 2. What was built — `_bay_content_pose` (styling.py)

- **Pose per cell, derived against the cell's own datums** (gables in width, the piece's front in
  depth) — R9: an alignment to a named datum, never a coordinate, never a nudge. **The front is
  derived by `millwork.mill_axis` and now rides on the anchor**; it did not before, which is why
  the old code could only centre. Front unknown, or derived onto the wrong axis → the solver
  **says so and centres in depth** rather than guessing a side.
- Pose steps by a stride coprime with the pose count → **no two adjacent cells share a pose**.
- Footprint **0.56 × 0.70 = 39.2%**, landing on the one fill figure the delivered-work survey
  states (*"each shelf keeps ~60% air"* — ONE frame, so a bracket, not a measurement).
- Vacancy stays at **~1/3** (his own *"บางช่องเข้าใจได้ว่ามันว่าง"*, and the delivered figure) but the
  empty slot **shifts along the run** instead of being every second cell. The first formula wrapped
  and emptied cells 8 and 9 back to back at a 12-cell run — an arithmetic accident, not a design
  choice — and the second one holds the third with no doubles.
- **A fraction needs a denominator**: the first cut applied one-in-three to a run of ONE cell and
  emptied it — 100% vacancy printed as 33%. The wardrobe unit test caught it immediately.
- **A companion group** in every other dressed cell, posed at the other end of the same cell, so a
  bay reads 1–2 items (the delivered band) — and it is the remedy the survey states in its own
  words: *"two more objects per column would close it"*.

**8 new tests, every one able to fail** (adjacent poses differ · centre is not the default · the
front is derived and its absence is not guessed · a cross-axis front is refused · vacancy is a
third with no doubles · a short run stays full · the fill really rose · companions exist and never
sit on the main pile). 69 pass in `test_styling.py`.
