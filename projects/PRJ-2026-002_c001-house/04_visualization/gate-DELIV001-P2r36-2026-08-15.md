## 2. THE ROOT CAUSE — A SCALE ASSERTION COPIED FROM THE FILE WE REJECTED

`master-suite.CANONICAL.spec.json:796` describes `ub806591a`:

> *"Scale ASSERTED as bedding_set: **2198.1 mm on y** … The file also carries a **2 m junk cube, a 12-poly mattress slab and pillow-scale parts**"*

The file's own sidecar, written by our own ingest and correct the whole time:

```
"measured_mm": 1599.9,  "bbox_mm": {x 1505.4, y 1599.9, z 396.8},  "prims": 6
```

**The spec is describing a different file.** 2198.1 mm on y is `2b142a32` — the
candidate this round REJECTED — and its `<mesh 5>` is the literal 12-poly
mattress slab at 2027.1 x 1524.2 mm. `ub806591a` holds **2 meshes / 6 prims /
5,924 tris and every one of them is cloth**: Mesh_0 the duvet with its
turned-back top sheet, Mesh_1 the turned-down runner. No cube, no slab, no
pillows.

### What that error produced, in millimetres

```
duvet as modelled      1505.4 x 1496.8 mm   =  52.4% of our mattress in plan
build upscale 1.236x   1860.7 x 1850.0 mm
our mattress           1999.6 x 2148.8 mm   (ink-trued, spec:785)
                          short 139 mm         short 299 mm
```

The duvet **cannot reach the mattress edges.** Its hem lands on the mattress TOP
FACE instead of falling past it, so the two surfaces occupy the same space along
the near corner — which is the sawtooth and the spike. It is also why
`bed__mattress` is still 2.51% of the rendered frame with bought bedding on it.

**Two guards existed for exactly this and neither fired.** `pipeline/CLAUDE.md`:
*"a plausible-but-wrong-scale model = the exact failure we sell against … add a
scale assertion on every ingest."* The assertion RAN and was RIGHT; the spec
recorded another file's number beside it, and the part-cut rule was written
against that other file's contents. **A correct measurement with a wrong number
copied next to it is worse than no measurement — it reads as diligence.**

## 4. AN INSTRUMENT BUG FOUND AND FIXED MID-ROUND

`look_bench.pick()` seeded the anchor panel from the **filename**, while its own
docstring claimed the purpose was *"so a before/after of OUR frame is judged
against an identical delivered panel"* — and a before/after always has two
filenames. The first A/B therefore drew two different panels; our frame scored
6/6 on one and 4/6 on the other and the builder nearly reported the jump as
progress. **Flattering scorer #15, self-inflicted.**

FIXED: `--panel-from` pins the draw to an explicit key; default behaviour and all
11 tests unchanged; `blind_slot` deliberately still seeds off the real filename so
our tile is not in a constant cell across sheets.

## 6. DECIDED, AND HOW TO REVERSE IT

1. **`_BED_CLOTH_ACQ` stays False.** Reverse: `--bed-cloth-acq`. The reason on
   record changes from *"the acquired frame is flatter"* to *"the asset is
   half-scale for this bed and the spec asserted another file's size"*.
2. **`ub806591a` is REFUSED for this bed** on measurement, not on look: 1505 x
   1497 mm of cloth cannot dress a 1999.6 x 2148.8 mm mattress. Reverse: a bed
   cloth whose own plan is ≥ the mattress.
3. **`look_bench --panel-from` is now required** for any two-frame comparison.
   Reverse: omit the flag.
4. **NOT decided, and deliberately left open:** whether a correctly-scaled
   acquired bed cloth beats our solver. **This round did not test it and must not
   be read as having tested it.**
