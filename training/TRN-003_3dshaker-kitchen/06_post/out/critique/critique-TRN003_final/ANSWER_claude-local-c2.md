# ANSWER_claude-local-c2 — TRN003_final

## TOP 3 (ranked)

1st → item 1. The frame is a kitchen with no kitchen in it: stone counter, full-height splash and bar stools, and not one sink, tap, hob, extractor, appliance, door, drawer line or handle.
2nd → item 2. Nothing in the room casts a shadow on the floor, and nothing has a contact darkening at its base. Every object is pasted onto the floor plane.
3rd → item 3. The sheer at the window is a flat card with zero folds carrying a tiled repeat of identical hard-edged bell shapes.

## DEFECTS

1. Not one working kitchen fitting exists anywhere in the frame. There is a stone counter, a full-height splash panel behind it and three bar stools pulled up to it — every signal that this is a kitchen — and then no sink, no tap, no hob, no extractor, no oven, no appliance of any kind, no cabinet door, no drawer line, no handle, no socket and no switch. The counter run and the island are blank extruded solids. A counter of this kind is defined by the fittings cut into it; a run with none has never been installed anywhere.
   where: the whole counter run and island, from the window at left across the centre of frame to the room corner at right
   stage: scaffolding · ground: physics · sellability: high

2. Nothing casts a shadow on the floor. The window at the left is blazing hard enough to print crisp shapes onto the curtain, so there is a strong directional key — yet the three stools, the two island columns and the counter box all throw no cast shadow whatsoever across the pale floor, and none of them has any darkening where it meets it. The stool feet in particular sit on the floor like stickers.
   where: the floor across the whole lower third — beside and to the right of the stools, and at the base of both island columns
   stage: lighting · ground: physics · sellability: high

3. The sheer curtain is a flat printed plane, not cloth. It has no folds, no gathers, no catenary sag at the head, no hem, no edge thickness and no variation in transmission anywhere across its width. Its "pattern" is a row-and-column repeat of identical hard-edged trapezoid/bell shapes that never bend, never overlap, never foreshorten and never break across a fold. Whether it is meant to be a printed sheer or the shadow of something outside thrown through the glass, no fabric hanging under its own weight and no shadow cast through a window produces that.
   where: the window wall, left third of frame, from under the dark beam down to where the counter crosses it
   stage: materials · ground: physics · sellability: high

4. The frame is blown out to the point where the room's own corners cannot be located. The entire left half, the whole ceiling and the top of the back wall sit at paper-white with no value separation between them. Top-left, the ceiling/wall corner is simply not there. Top-right, the join between the white wall and the peach wall is a soft gradient, not an edge. A client cannot read the shape of the room they are being sold.
   where: upper half of frame — the ceiling, the top of the white back wall, and the whole left window field
   stage: lighting · ground: physics · sellability: high

5. The room is unstyled to the point of reading as an unfinished model. The counter top carries nothing at all — no bowl, no board, no glass, no plant, no book, no cloth. The run behind carries nothing. The floor carries nothing. There is one panel on the wall and three stools, and that is the entire contents of the picture.
   where: the whole island top and the counter run behind it, centre of frame; the floor lower-right
   stage: composition · ground: opinion · sellability: high

6. The stool seats sit almost level with the underside of the counter — there is essentially no lap clearance. The seat plates read only a little below the counter's top surface, and the counter slab is thick, so the space left between a seat and the underside of the slab is a fraction of a thigh. Nobody could get their legs under it and sit.
   where: the two nearest stools, lower-left, against the left end of the counter
   stage: geometry · ground: physics · sellability: high

7. The dark beam over the window has a rippled, blobby underside. A lintel, bulkhead or curtain pocket is cast, extruded or built out of board; every one of those produces a straight, level soffit.
   where: the dark grey beam spanning the top of the window, upper-left
   stage: geometry · ground: physics · sellability: high

## CANNOT-BE-BUILT

These could not exist as manufactured, installed objects. For each: what it rests on, or that nothing does.

* The dark panel under the counter (item 14) — **nothing.** Its bottom edge stops with no plinth, no floor line, no contact shadow and no visible support. If it does land on the floor, the frame contains no evidence of it.
* The right-hand end of the stone top (item 25) — **nothing.** Past the second ochre column the slab cantilevers about two column widths and terminates in air; there is no bracket, no gable, no rail, no return.
* The sheer curtain (item 3) — **nothing visible.** No track, rod, ring, tape, header or fixing appears anywhere in frame; its head vanishes behind the beam, so inside this picture the cloth hangs off the beam's rippled underside, which is not a fixing.
* The beam's soffit over the window (item 7) — carried by the ceiling above, but as drawn its underside is a wavering lumpy cast. No formwork, no extrusion, no board and no joinery produces that edge; it is not a manufacturable part regardless of what holds it up.
* The pale splash slab behind the counter (items 12, 15) — **unresolvable.** Its top edge stops mid-wall with no return or trim and its right-hand side never resolves against the room corner, so the picture cannot say what it is fixed to or whether it is in front of, behind, or part of the wall.
* Every cabinet and counter in the frame (items 1, 13) — solid extruded boxes. No door, no drawer, no reveal, no hinge, no handle, no carcass joint, no plinth. A box with no openings is not a cabinet; it is a mass standing where one should be.
