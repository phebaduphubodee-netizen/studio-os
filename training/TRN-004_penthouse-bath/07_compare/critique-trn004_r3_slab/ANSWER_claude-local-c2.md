# ANSWER — C2 cold critic (fresh context, bundle-only)

**What I was handed, and what I did not open.** The bundle directory contains
`PROMPT.md` and `trn004_r3_slab.png` — I read both. It also contains
`README.md`, `OWNER-NOTE-not-part-of-the-ask.md`, and a nested `c2-ask/`
subdirectory holding a second copy of `PROMPT.md` and a second copy of the PNG.
I opened none of those. No reference, target or anchor image was given and I did
not go looking for one — this is a render-only judgment, and every `ground:
physics` tag below means physical common sense for a built object of that kind,
not a comparison. No sibling `ANSWER_*.md` was present in the bundle dir.

**One thing the builder needs to know about the rung itself.** The text ABOVE
the `---` separator in `PROMPT.md` is process history and it reaches a judge that
is supposed to be blind: it narrates what an earlier C2 run caught, that a
previous version of the file leaked a worked example of a past defect, and which
laws were amended because of it. It does not name this render's build, so it did
not hand me a defect to hunt — but a blind rung whose prompt tells the judge how
previous judges performed is not fully blind, and that is a defect in the rung.

## TOP 3 (ranked — these are items 1, 2 and 3 below)

- **TOP-1 — There is no sanitaryware anywhere in the frame.** No basin, no tap,
  no waste, no shower fitting, no drain, no towel bar. A client cannot read this
  picture as a bathroom.
- **TOP-2 — The glazed enclosure has no door and no way in**, and no tray, curb,
  fall or drain under it.
- **TOP-3 — Nothing in the frame casts a shadow and no light fixture exists**;
  the room reads as an ambient-occlusion preview, not a photographed room.

---

1. **No sanitaryware exists in the room at all.** where: the grey slab running
   along the left wall from the bottom-left corner up to centre frame, and the
   whole floor plane. The slab is unbroken grey for its entire visible length —
   no bowl, no undermount cutout, no tap hole, no spout, no mixer, no waste, no
   bottle trap, no isolation valves. Nowhere in the frame is there a shower head,
   riser, hand shower, thermostat, WC, bidet spray, floor gully, towel bar, ring,
   hook or paper holder. A bathroom is defined by its fittings; a room with none
   of them is a lobby ledge. This is the single largest thing between this frame
   and a sale.
   stage: scaffolding · ground: physics · sellability: high

2. **The glazed enclosure cannot be entered and cannot hold water.** where:
   centre-right, the black-framed glass running from just past the counter's far
   end across to the right-hand wall, floor to ceiling. It is one continuous
   frame around four fixed panels — there is no door leaf, no handle, no knob, no
   hinge, no pivot, no roller track, no head track, no meeting stile, and no gap
   anywhere in the perimeter. Underneath it, the same floor tile runs straight
   through from outside to inside with no upstand, no curb, no threshold section,
   no visible fall and no drain of any kind. As drawn it is a sealed glass box: a
   person cannot get in and water cannot get out.
   stage: scaffolding · ground: physics · sellability: high

3. **Nothing casts a shadow, and the light has no source.** where: whole frame —
   specifically the floor beneath the counter's front face (lower-left third),
   the counter surface beside and behind the towel stack, and the floor in front
   of the glazed bay. The towels sit on the slab with no directional shadow. The
   counter throws nothing on the floor. The glazed frame throws nothing. There is
   no luminaire anywhere: no downlight, no ceiling fitting, no cove, no mirror
   light, no vanity light, no wall washer. The only bright surface is the glass,
   yet the right-hand wall does not fall off away from it — it gets brighter
   toward the right frame edge, which is backwards for a single glowing source.
   Light that arrives from nothing and leaves no shadow reads to any client as an
   unfinished preview.
   stage: lighting · ground: physics · sellability: high

4. **Every surface is flat colour with no material identity, and the tone
   changes in hard-edged rectangular patches.** where: the right-hand wall,
   especially the large distinctly darker/greyer rectangle occupying the
   upper-right quarter of the frame between the glazed frame and the right edge;
   also the ceiling above and left of the glazed head; also the floor. The "stone"
   panels have no veining, no figure, no book-match, no directional grain and no
   grout or shadow-gap joint — panels are told apart only by a step in tone, and
   those steps land on hard rectangular boundaries that no stone or tile does.
   The floor has no grout, no bevel, no relief and no reflection. The whole room
   reads as untextured masses with tone variation painted on.
   stage: materials · ground: physics · sellability: high

5. **The left wall cannot be identified — it is unreadable as either a mirror or
   a second glazed opening.** where: the left third of the frame above the
   counter, where the four dark-framed frosted panels stop and beige stone
   begins. There is no mirror edge, no frame, no polished arris, no silicone
   joint, no shadow gap and no change of plane at that boundary; the panels
   simply end. If it is a mirror, then it is reflecting the glass wall but is
   showing no image of the grey counter slab and no image of the folded towel
   stack standing right against it — a mirror reflects everything in front of it
   or it is not a mirror. If it is glazing, then it is a second window with no
   frame and no reveal. Either reading fails, and a client looking at the largest
   plane on the left of the picture cannot tell what it is made of.
   stage: scaffolding · ground: physics · sellability: high

6. **The towels are rigid solids, not cloth.** where: on the counter, left of
   centre — the stack of three and the roll beside it. The folded stack is three
   slabs of identical thickness with dead-straight parallel edges, uniform
   corner radii on all four corners, and even gaps between layers; real folded
   terry sags at the unsupported edge, the bottom towel compresses under the ones
   above, the fold rolls over instead of ending in a straight line, and no two
   edges in a stack line up. The roll is a perfect extruded cylinder with a flat
   cap: no spiral, no visible free end of the towel, no slump where it rests, no
   flare at the ends. Neither shows any terry pile, weave, hem, selvedge or
   border band, and neither deforms at all where it touches the slab.
   stage: materials · ground: physics · sellability: high

7. **The room is styled to almost nothing.** where: the whole counter run from
   the bottom-left corner to centre frame, and the entire floor. Two towel items
   is the complete inventory of the picture. There is no soap or amenity bottle,
   no tray, no dish, no plant or stem, no stool or bench, no basket, no folded
   robe, no candle, no art, no book, nothing on the floor but one grey rectangle.
   A penthouse bathroom presented to a client is sold on exactly this layer, and
   the frame reads as a construction shot taken before handover styling.
   stage: composition · ground: physics · sellability: high

8. **There is nothing behind the frosted glass.** where: the four lit panels of
   the enclosure, centre-right, and their counterparts on the left wall. Each
   panel is a smooth uninterrupted vertical gradient, essentially identical from
   panel to panel, containing no silhouette of anything at all — no shower riser,
   no arm, no bench, no niche, no fixture, no ceiling line, no horizon, no
   variation of the obscure pattern between panes. Obscured glass in a real room
   always carries the blurred dark shape of whatever is behind it. As rendered
   the panels read as emissive light boxes rather than glass with a space behind
   them, which is also why the enclosure has no identity: the picture never tells
   you whether that volume is a shower, a steam room or a window.
   stage: materials · ground: physics · sellability: high

9. **The vanity ledge reads too low for a vanity.** where: the grey slab, taken
   at its far end where it meets the glazed frame, measured against the
   floor-to-ceiling height at that same depth. The top of the slab sits at
   roughly a quarter of the wall height there; a vanity at 850-900 mm in a room
   of 2.4-3.0 m sits at a third or more, and the counter's far end is if anything
   nearer the camera than the wall I measured against, so the true height is
   lower still. Against the towels on it, it reads as a bench or a window sill,
   not a surface you stand at to wash. Combined with item 1, the mass has neither
   the height nor the fittings of the thing it is standing in for.
   stage: geometry · ground: physics · sellability: high

10. **The vanity is a solid tiled block, not joinery.** where: the front face of
    the counter, running from the bottom-left corner to centre frame. It is one
    unbroken tiled mass from the slab straight down to the floor: no drawer
    fronts, no doors, no shadow gap, no reveal, no handles or push-catches, no
    end panel, no visible carcass line, and no toe recess at the floor. Without a
    toe recess a person cannot stand close enough to use the surface, and without
    any opening there is nowhere for a waste, a trap or a supply to run — the
    block would have to be demolished to service anything.
    stage: geometry · ground: physics · sellability: medium

11. **The rolled towel is not resting on the counter.** where: the white cylinder
    at the upper-left of the counter run, above and to the left of the folded
    stack. It sits higher in frame than the stack while being further LEFT along
    the counter — and left along this counter means nearer the camera, which for
    an object on the same surface must put it LOWER in frame, not higher. The
    counter's own back-edge line passes well below it. Nothing at all is visible
    underneath it: no shelf, no bracket, no ledge, no upstand, no second tier.
    It is either floating against the left wall or it is a reflection whose
    source object does not exist in the picture.
    stage: geometry · ground: physics · sellability: medium

12. **The grey rectangle on the floor has no thickness and no contact.** where:
    the floor, lower-centre-right, directly in front of the glazed bay. Its edges
    are perfectly hard lines with no bevel, no pile, no hem, no fringe and no
    visible cross-section; there is no darkening where it meets the tile and no
    shadow along any edge; and it is exactly the same grey as the counter slab.
    A textile mat has thickness and lifts light at its edge; an inset panel or a
    drain has a reveal, a frame or a grating. This has none of the three, so it
    reads as a grey shape painted onto the floor rather than an object lying on
    it. It is also not aligned to the floor joints, nor centred on the glazed bay
    it apparently serves.
    stage: materials · ground: physics · sellability: medium

13. **The counter's far end and the enclosure's corner post occupy the same
    place with no junction.** where: centre-left, where the grey slab terminates
    against the tall black post at the left-hand end of the glazed bay. The slab
    runs right up to the post and appears to pass into it: there is no scribe, no
    reveal, no end cap, no upstand, no silicone joint, no shadow gap, and no gap
    for the glass channel that the post would need. Two built assemblies cannot
    share the same millimetre; either the counter has to stop short of the screen
    or the screen has to be trimmed around it, and the frame shows neither.
    stage: geometry · ground: physics · sellability: medium

14. **The counter slab is dead — it reflects nothing.** where: the grey top
    surface, from the bottom-left corner to centre frame. The brightest object in
    the room is a full-height glowing glass wall standing directly opposite it,
    and there is not a trace of it on the slab: no gradient, no sheen, no
    softened reflection of the towels, no specular roll-off toward the far end.
    The slab also shows no edge profile at the front — no eased arris, no
    thickness, no shadow line under the overhang. It is the same flat grey as the
    floor rectangle in item 12, which makes both read as the same untextured
    default rather than two chosen finishes.
    stage: materials · ground: physics · sellability: medium

15. **The black metalwork has no thickness and no metal in it.** where: the
    frame of the glazed enclosure, in particular the corner post at the left end
    of the bay and the two central mullions. Every member is a uniform matte
    black stripe: the corner post turns a corner but its two faces are the same
    black, with no lighter return face and no highlight on the arris; there is no
    glazing bead, no gasket line, no silicone bead, no fixing, and no specular
    anywhere along a member that is standing beside a bright source. Anodised or
    powder-coated aluminium always carries a graded highlight down its length.
    As drawn it reads as black tape laid over the glass.
    stage: materials · ground: physics · sellability: medium

16. **No upstand or backsplash where the wet slab meets the wall.** where: the
    back edge of the counter, along its whole visible length, where the grey slab
    meets the left-hand plane. The slab dies straight into the wall/mirror plane
    with an open butt joint — no upstand, no seal, no silicone line, no shadow
    gap. Water at a vanity runs into that joint; every built wet counter closes it.
    stage: geometry · ground: physics · sellability: medium

17. **The composition has no subject.** where: the whole frame. The only
    designed object — the counter with its towels — is cut in half by the left
    frame edge and is never seen whole; roughly the middle and right 40% of the
    picture is an empty glowing rectangle containing nothing; the right-hand third
    is bare wall. There is no focal point, nothing at eye level to land on, and no
    depth cue other than the receding wall panels. A client's eye leaves this
    frame without having been given anything to look at.
    stage: composition · ground: opinion · sellability: medium

18. **The palette is a single beige.** where: the whole frame — right wall,
    ceiling, floor, counter front, and the reflected wall on the left are all the
    same warm off-white, broken only by the black frame and one grey. There is no
    accent, no second material, no warm/cool play, no stone with any character in
    it, and the only colour event in the picture is the cool blue cast in the
    glass panels. For work presented as a penthouse bathroom this reads as
    undecided rather than restrained.
    stage: materials · ground: opinion · sellability: medium

19. **No ventilation and no electrical anywhere.** where: the ceiling above the
    glazed bay and along the left wall, and the wall surfaces around the counter.
    There is no extract grille, no fan, no vent, no linear slot, and no access
    panel; there is no switch plate, no socket, no shaver point, no isolator, and
    no light circuit at all. A wet room with a full-height glazed steam or shower
    volume cannot be built without extract, and a vanity cannot be built without
    at least a switched circuit.
    stage: scaffolding · ground: physics · sellability: medium

20. **The tile and panel setout is arbitrary.** where: the right-hand wall
    (panels of visibly different heights and widths, an L-shaped patch below the
    dark rectangle), the ceiling (rectangular tone patches that do not follow any
    grid), and the floor (joints that do not align with either wall). Panel joints
    do not line up between wall, floor and ceiling; the wall is not set out
    symmetrically about the glazed bay or about the counter; joint widths are
    invisible in some places and implied in others; and no course is centred on
    anything. Large-format wall panelling is set out from a datum on a real job,
    and the eye reads a setout that was not.
    stage: materials · ground: physics · sellability: medium

## CANNOT-BE-BUILT — things that could not exist as manufactured, installed objects

- **The glazed enclosure (item 2)** — a continuous frame, floor to ceiling, wall
  to wall, around four fixed panels, with no leaf, no hinge, no pivot, no track
  and no gap in its perimeter. Nothing opens. What it rests on: the floor tile,
  directly, with no threshold section, no tray and no curb — and the same tile
  runs through unchanged, so there is no drain the enclosure could ever discharge
  to. It cannot be entered and it cannot be waterproofed.
- **The rolled towel, upper-left of the counter (item 11)** — nothing is under
  it. No shelf, no bracket, no ledge, no second tier, no upstand. It stands above
  the counter's own back edge line, further along the run than a resting object
  could be, supported by nothing.
- **The grey rectangle on the floor, lower-centre-right (item 12)** — zero
  thickness, no edge cross-section, no contact darkening. It is not resting on
  the floor; it is coincident with the floor plane. A textile of no thickness and
  an inset of no reveal are both unmanufacturable.
- **The light in the room (item 3)** — there is no luminaire, no lamp, no
  fitting, no cove and no aperture other than the glass. The illumination is
  carried by nothing, and it deposits no shadow from any object that intercepts
  it.
- **The left-wall plane (item 5)** — a reflective panel that ends without an edge,
  a frame, a polished arris or a joint, and that shows some of what stands in
  front of it and not the rest. A mirror is a manufactured panel with a perimeter
  and it reflects everything; this has neither property, so as drawn there is no
  product it could be.
- **The counter end at the glazed corner post (item 13)** — two assemblies
  sharing the same location with no junction detail between them; the slab
  appears to be resting inside the post rather than stopping against it.
- **The vanity block (item 10)** — a sealed tiled mass with no access opening,
  so there is no route for a waste, a trap or a supply, and no service access
  after installation; and no toe recess, so nobody can stand at it.
